# PM Acceptance Criteria for Buy-Side Research Reports

## Purpose

This reference defines the quality standard for a buy-side research report that is good enough for a Portfolio Manager.

The report must not be a generic industry summary. It must convert research into a clear, evidence-backed, actionable view while showing the limits of the thesis.

This file is a quality standard only. It does not generate an actual report.

## Core PM Questions

A PM-ready report must answer all of the following questions:

1. What changed?
2. Why now?
3. Why does it matter?
4. Who benefits?
5. What does the market believe?
6. Where is the market wrong?
7. What could invalidate this thesis?
8. What should the PM do next?

If any question is unanswered, the report is incomplete.

## Quality Requirements

### 1. Thesis First

The report must open with the core thesis before background context.

The thesis should state:

- the key change or mispricing
- the affected industry, company, or value chain segment
- the expected direction of impact
- the timeframe that matters
- the strongest reason the PM should care now

Avoid opening with broad market history, generic definitions, or undifferentiated industry background.

### 2. Evidence Chain

Every major claim must connect evidence to investment relevance.

Use this chain:

`claim -> source evidence -> verification status -> business impact -> market implication`

Evidence should be specific enough that another analyst can trace the logic. Unsupported assertions, vague expert-sounding language, and orphan facts should be removed or marked as `待补充`.

### 3. Multi-Source Verification

Important claims must be checked across multiple source types when possible.

Preferred verification pattern:

- primary or official source for facts
- financial or operating data for magnitude
- market or channel signal for timing
- management commentary or filings for company relevance

X, Hacker News, Product Hunt, and similar sources can support early signal discovery, but they cannot be treated as final proof without validation from stronger sources.

### 4. Company Mapping

The report must map the theme to investable companies or relevant private companies.

For each important company, identify:

- role in the value chain
- source of exposure
- expected benefit or risk
- revenue, margin, cost, or valuation linkage where available
- key data gaps

The report should distinguish direct beneficiaries from indirect, speculative, or narrative-driven beneficiaries.

### 5. Catalyst Analysis

The report must explain what can cause the market to update.

Catalysts may include:

- earnings results
- guidance changes
- product launches
- regulatory decisions
- capacity additions
- pricing changes
- customer wins or losses
- supply chain shifts
- financing, IPO, M&A, or capital return events

For each major catalyst, state the expected timing, observable indicator, and thesis relevance.

### 6. Market Expectation Analysis

The report must describe what the market already believes.

It should identify:

- consensus expectations
- prevailing narrative
- sell-side or investor debate
- valuation assumptions
- positioning or sentiment indicators where available
- what appears priced in

Do not claim the market is wrong without first describing the market's current belief.

### 7. Bear Case / Invalidation

The report must include a serious bear case and explicit invalidation conditions.

Invalidation should include:

- facts that would disprove the thesis
- data points that would weaken the expected magnitude
- timing risks
- competitive risks
- regulatory or policy risks
- execution risks
- valuation risks

A good invalidation section should help the PM know what to monitor after reading the report.

### 8. Actionability

The report must end with a clear next step for the PM.

Acceptable actionability includes:

- initiate deeper work on named companies
- add companies to watchlist
- monitor named catalysts or data points
- compare valuation assumptions against consensus
- prepare diligence questions for management, experts, suppliers, or customers
- size follow-up research priority

Actionability does not require a final buy/sell decision, but it must make the next research or portfolio action obvious.

## Acceptance Checklist

Before a report is considered PM-ready, confirm:

- The thesis appears before background context.
- The report directly answers all eight core PM questions.
- Major claims have source-backed evidence chains.
- Important facts are verified with stronger sources when available.
- Early signals are separated from verified facts.
- Company exposure is mapped clearly.
- Catalysts are specific, observable, and time-bound where possible.
- Market expectations are described before variant perception is argued.
- Bear case and invalidation conditions are concrete.
- The final section tells the PM what to do next.
- Missing data is marked as `待补充`, `待验证`, or `数据暂缺`.
- No fabricated sources, metrics, URLs, rankings, claims, or company conclusions are included.

## Automatic Failure Conditions

A report fails this standard if it:

- reads like a generic industry overview
- delays the thesis until the end
- lists facts without explaining why they matter
- treats early X or social signals as verified facts
- lacks company-level mapping
- lacks market expectation analysis
- lacks a bear case or invalidation criteria
- recommends action without evidence
- invents sources, metrics, claims, URLs, rankings, or market views
- hides key uncertainty instead of marking it clearly

## Minimum Standard

A PM-ready report should leave the PM with:

1. a clear thesis
2. a traceable evidence chain
3. a differentiated view versus market expectations
4. a company-level map of winners and losers
5. catalysts to monitor
6. explicit invalidation criteria
7. a concrete next step
