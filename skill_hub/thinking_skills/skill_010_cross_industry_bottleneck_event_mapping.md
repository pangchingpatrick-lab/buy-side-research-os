# Skill 010: Cross-Industry Bottleneck Event Mapping

This thinking skill maps public research events into a constrained-layer thesis that can be tested across industries.

It is the cross-industry companion to:

- `skill_hub/core_skills/skill_004_serenity_bottleneck_mapping.md`
- `skill_hub/core_skills/skill_005_research_event_distillation.md`
- `skill_hub/thinking_skills/skill_009_research_event_reasoning.md`

## Core Formula

```text
demand shock
-> constrained layer
-> scarce capability
-> underpriced beneficiary
-> falsifiable event
-> benchmark / price-path audit
```

The goal is not to copy AI or semiconductor terminology. The goal is to find where a real constraint turns a broad theme into company-level economics.

## Use When

Use this skill when the packet asks:

- who benefits from a new demand shock
- which layer of the value chain is the real bottleneck
- whether a public signal identifies an overlooked beneficiary
- whether a theme is too broad and needs a constrained-layer map
- how to generalize an AI/semi bottleneck method to energy, software, space, robotics, industrials, healthcare, consumer electronics, or financial infrastructure

## Thinking Chain

### 1. Define The Demand Shock

State what changed.

Examples:

- architecture transition
- capex cycle
- customer deployment
- regulation or policy shift
- product cycle
- replacement cycle
- supply disruption
- pricing or inventory turn
- usage acceleration

Reject vague drivers like "AI is growing" unless tied to a specific workload, deployment, order, budget, product, or customer behavior.

### 2. Map The Value Chain

Break the theme into:

- customers
- platforms / integrators
- component suppliers
- equipment vendors
- materials
- logistics / services
- financing or regulatory gatekeepers

Mark which companies are obvious consensus winners and which ones are less visible enablers.

### 3. Identify The Constrained Layer

Look for:

- long lead time
- limited qualified capacity
- yield issues
- scarce materials
- customer approval or certification
- high switching cost
- regulatory bottleneck
- specialized labor
- power, cooling, packaging, memory, test, or infrastructure constraints

A real bottleneck should explain pricing power, delayed supply response, margin pressure, or operating leverage.

### 4. Identify The Scarce Capability

Name the capability, not only the company.

Examples:

- memory bandwidth
- advanced packaging
- optical interconnect
- transformer / switchgear capacity
- launch cadence
- robotics actuation
- cold chain manufacturing
- cybersecurity distribution
- customer qualification channel

This prevents ticker-first reasoning.

### 5. Map Beneficiary Proximity

Classify companies:

- direct owner of scarce capability
- second-order supplier
- customer demand validator
- peer / comparison
- obvious large-cap proxy
- potential loser
- watchlist only

Prefer beneficiaries with direct exposure, financial sensitivity, and evidence that the market may not fully price.

### 6. Build A Falsifiable Event

Turn the thesis into a testable event:

```text
source
timestamp
claim
affected company / node
expected mechanism
time horizon
evidence stack
invalidation condition
benchmark or peer basket
```

If this cannot be done, keep the idea as a watchlist topic rather than a learned rule.

### 7. Audit Price-In And Benchmark

Ask:

- Did the source appear before the move?
- Was there prior runup?
- Is this early discovery, continuation confirmation, late validation, after-fact commentary, or context only?
- What benchmark should be used for relative return?
- Is a broad benchmark hiding a specific bottleneck?

Use industry-appropriate benchmarks or peer baskets. Semiconductor and AI infrastructure may use SOXX as a reference; other sectors need their own comparison set.

## Guardrails

- Do not turn a broad theme into a ticker call without the constrained layer.
- Do not treat customers, peers, or mega-cap proxies as direct beneficiaries by default.
- Do not call an underpriced beneficiary unless timing, evidence, and market expectation are addressed.
- Do not use one source item as a permanent rule.
- Do not learn ticker-specific recommendations; learn reusable bottleneck-search behavior.
