# Skill 005: Narrative Reframing

## 1. Skill purpose

Turn a broad or popular market narrative into a specific investable mechanism.

## 2. When to use

Use when:

- the research starts from a vague narrative
- a topic is popular but not decision-useful
- the report needs to separate hype from operating reality
- the same signal can be interpreted through multiple frames
- the PM needs a sharper question before acting

## 3. Input required

- current market narrative
- early signals
- related industry framework
- company list
- verified evidence
- known data gaps
- market expectation context if available

## 4. Reasoning steps

1. State the current narrative in plain language.
2. Identify why the narrative is too broad or misleading.
3. Reframe it into a mechanism that can be tested.
4. Identify the operating metrics linked to the reframed mechanism.
5. Map companies exposed to the mechanism.
6. Define what evidence would validate or reject the reframed view.
7. Translate the reframed view into PM action choices.

## 5. Output expected

- old narrative
- reframed narrative
- testable mechanism
- metrics to check
- company exposure
- validation questions
- PM action implication

## 6. Example use case

Instead of "AI demand is growing," reframe to "inference adoption is increasing data center power density and networking requirements, shifting marginal economics toward specific infrastructure bottlenecks."

## MRVL case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

A company should not always be framed by its product label. A stronger frame may be its role in the customer's system architecture, such as an AI datacenter interconnect and custom silicon platform provider.

### Reusable reasoning rule

Reframe a company from its product label to its role in the customer's system architecture.

### Example pattern

```text
old frame: product vendor
new frame: architecture-enabling platform
testable mechanism: design wins, custom silicon growth, interconnect content, optical roadmap, margin profile
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not reframe a company just to make the story sound bigger. The new frame must create testable financial indicators and explain customer value.

## MSSCorps case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

The stronger frame may be to reclassify a company from "material / failure analysis lab" to "CPO inspection monopoly and yield chokepoint."

### Reusable reasoning rule

Reclassify a company by the bottleneck it controls, not by its surface-level industry label.

### Example pattern

```text
old frame: surface industry label
new frame: bottleneck controlled
testable mechanism: customer dependency, inspection adoption, yield relevance, pricing power, litigation risk
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not use a stronger label unless it can be tested. The reframing must be supported by evidence of customer dependency, substitute scarcity, IP position, and measurable financial contribution.

## 7. Common mistakes

- replacing one vague narrative with another
- making a thesis sound contrarian without evidence
- failing to define testable metrics
- ignoring who captures economics
- using the reframe as a conclusion instead of a hypothesis
