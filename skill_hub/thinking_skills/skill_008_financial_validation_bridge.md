# Skill 008: Financial Validation Bridge

## 1. Skill purpose

Convert qualitative signals into specific financial validation questions.

This skill bridges thinking skills and analysis methods.

## 2. When to use

Use when:

- a signal or thesis exists but financial proof is incomplete
- the report needs to move from narrative to measurable data
- early X or expert signals need validation from filings, earnings calls, guidance, or financial data
- the PM needs to know what to check next

## 3. Input required

- signal or thesis
- affected companies
- operating mechanism
- relevant industry framework
- available financial evidence
- missing data
- verification status

## 4. Reasoning steps

1. State the qualitative claim.
2. Identify the financial statement line item that should move.
3. Identify the operating metric that connects the claim to the financial line.
4. Identify the source that can validate the metric.
5. Define the expected evidence if the claim is true.
6. Define the contrary evidence if the claim is false.
7. Convert each gap into a validation question.
8. Route the question to the appropriate analysis method.

## 5. Output expected

- qualitative claim
- financial line item
- operating metric
- data source to check
- expected confirming evidence
- expected disconfirming evidence
- next validation questions
- recommended analysis method

## 6. Example use case

If the thesis is that optical networking benefits from AI cluster scale-out, the bridge asks which companies should show order growth, backlog, ASP strength, margin expansion, capex plans, or customer commentary in filings and earnings calls.

## MRVL case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

A platform narrative should be validated through measurable indicators such as data center revenue mix, custom silicon revenue growth, CPO contribution timeline, margin profile, design wins, customer concentration, and management guidance.

### Reusable reasoning rule

Translate platform narrative into measurable financial indicators before making investment judgment.

### Example pattern

```text
platform narrative
-> measurable business line or product category
-> revenue mix, growth, margin, design wins, customer concentration, and guidance
-> evidence from filings, earnings calls, investor presentations, and management commentary
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not validate a platform thesis with narrative strength alone. Require source-checkable financial and operating indicators.

## MSSCorps case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

A monopoly chokepoint thesis should be validated through customer evidence, inspection adoption, CPO ramp timing, pricing power, litigation outcome, yield relevance, and revenue / margin acceleration.

### Reusable reasoning rule

For monopoly chokepoint theses, validate through customer dependency, pricing power, margin expansion, and whether bottleneck demand converts into measurable financial growth.

### Example pattern

```text
monopoly chokepoint narrative
-> customer dependency and substitute availability
-> adoption timing and yield relevance
-> pricing power, revenue contribution, margin profile, and litigation risk
-> filings, earnings calls, customer evidence, and legal / IP disclosures
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not assume monopoly status only from narrative. Verify customer relationships, patent position, substitute availability, revenue contribution, and litigation risk.

## 7. Common mistakes

- asking vague validation questions
- treating social signals as financial proof
- failing to name the source to check
- validating the wrong metric
- ignoring negative evidence that would reject the thesis
