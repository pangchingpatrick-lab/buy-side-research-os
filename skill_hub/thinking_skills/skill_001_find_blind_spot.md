# Skill 001: Find Blind Spot

## 1. Skill purpose

Identify what the market may be overlooking, underweighting, or framing incorrectly.

This skill turns a research packet from "what happened" into "what might the market be missing?"

## 2. When to use

Use when:

- a topic is widely discussed but conclusions feel consensus
- early signals contradict common market narrative
- a company or value-chain node is affected indirectly
- the market focuses on the obvious winner while ignoring second-order beneficiaries or losers
- the report needs a variant view

Do not use when the research packet has no evidence, no company linkage, and no identifiable market narrative.

## 3. Input required

- research topic
- current market narrative or consensus assumption
- early signals
- verified evidence
- related companies
- known data gaps
- valuation or expectation context if available

## 4. Reasoning steps

1. State the obvious consensus view.
2. Identify what evidence the consensus likely relies on.
3. List what the consensus view ignores.
4. Separate ignored facts from unverified possibilities.
5. Ask whether the ignored point affects revenue, margin, orders, capex, inventory, EPS, valuation, or risk.
6. Map the blind spot to direct, indirect, and negative beneficiaries.
7. Define the evidence that would make the blind spot investable.
8. Define what would prove the blind spot irrelevant.

## 5. Output expected

- consensus view
- suspected blind spot
- why the blind spot matters
- affected companies or value-chain nodes
- required validation data
- invalidation evidence
- PM action state: ignore, monitor, research further, or act

## 6. Example use case

AI infrastructure discussion focuses on GPU demand. This skill asks whether the market is missing a constraint in upstream networking, optical modules, power, memory, or data center capacity that could shift profit pools away from the obvious winner.

## MRVL case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

A possible blind spot is that investors may apply a narrow market label to a company while missing its broader role in the value chain. In this pattern, the market label may be "generic semiconductor company," while the investable question is whether the company functions as a system-level AI interconnect platform.

### Reusable reasoning rule

Look for cases where the market label is too narrow relative to the company's actual role in the customer's system architecture or value chain.

### Example pattern

```text
market label
-> actual role in customer architecture
-> value-chain node controlled
-> financial metric that should reveal the role
-> evidence needed before upgrading conviction
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not call something a blind spot merely because the company has AI exposure. The blind spot must be a specific mismatch between market framing and the company's economic role.

## MSSCorps case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

The market may misclassify a company as a generic material or failure-analysis company, while the hidden exposure is its role in CPO inspection and yield control.

### Reusable reasoning rule

Look for companies whose market label understates their strategic role in an emerging technology bottleneck.

### Example pattern

```text
surface label
-> hidden bottleneck role
-> emerging technology dependency
-> yield, qualification, or reliability impact
-> source-checkable evidence needed
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not assume the market is missing something just because the company serves an exciting end market. The blind spot must be a specific mismatch between market label and strategic bottleneck role.

## 7. Common mistakes

- confusing a contrarian statement with a valid blind spot
- claiming the market is wrong without identifying consensus
- naming tickers without exposure mechanism
- treating unverified X signals as facts
- ignoring valuation and expectation context
