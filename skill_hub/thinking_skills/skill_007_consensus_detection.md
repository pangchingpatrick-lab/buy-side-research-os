# Skill 007: Consensus Detection

## 1. Skill purpose

Infer what the market likely believes, what is already priced in, and where consensus may be stale, incomplete, or wrong.

## 2. When to use

Use when:

- a report needs variant perception
- the PM asks whether the idea is priced in
- the topic is popular and narrative-heavy
- estimates, valuation, or positioning may already reflect the signal
- early signals conflict with market expectations

## 3. Input required

- market narrative
- valuation or price context if available
- consensus estimates or guidance if available
- early signals
- verified evidence
- company exposure
- key debates and data gaps

## 4. Reasoning steps

1. State what consensus likely believes.
2. Identify evidence consensus may be using.
3. Identify whether the signal is known, partially known, or ignored.
4. Determine whether consensus may be wrong on timing, magnitude, durability, margins, beneficiary mapping, or risk.
5. Compare consensus view with the emerging evidence.
6. Define what would force consensus to revise.
7. Define what would show consensus is already correct.

## 5. Output expected

- likely consensus view
- what is priced in
- what may not be priced in
- variant perception
- catalyst for consensus revision
- invalidation evidence
- PM action implication

## 6. Example use case

The market may know AI capex is high, but may underestimate which upstream constraints convert into supplier pricing power or overestimate the duration of demand for a crowded beneficiary.

## MRVL case refinement

This case is used only to strengthen the reusable thinking pattern. It is not a stock opinion or recommendation.

### What this case teaches

The market may already price in high expectations before earnings. The key question is not whether the story is attractive, but whether earnings call commentary strengthens or weakens the long-term AI platform thesis.

### Reusable reasoning rule

Before upgrading conviction, check whether the key thesis is already consensus and whether new information can still change expectations.

### Example pattern

```text
pre-earnings expectations
-> consensus thesis already priced in or not
-> new management commentary
-> estimate revision potential
-> conviction upgrade, hold, or downgrade
```

### Common mistake to avoid

Do not confuse a good long-term story with a mispriced story. Consensus detection must ask what is already embedded in expectations.

## 7. Common mistakes

- declaring "not priced in" without valuation or expectation context
- confusing personal disagreement with variant perception
- ignoring consensus estimates
- assuming popular topics cannot have blind spots
- failing to identify a catalyst for market recognition
