# Real Estate Framework

This is a buy-side real estate industry framework. It is designed for developers, REITs, data centers, logistics, office, retail property, property management, and homebuilder analysis.

Use it after external data has already been collected, classified, and source-tagged. Do not use this framework to create generic property-market commentary.

## 1. Industry Essence

Real estate is driven by asset value, rental income, occupancy, interest rates, leverage, financing conditions, property cycles, and policy.

The investable question is not "property demand is recovering." The investable question is:

- Where are we in the property cycle?
- Does the signal affect sales volume, rent, occupancy, cap rate, or refinancing risk?
- Are asset values marked realistically?
- Is leverage manageable through the next refinancing window?
- Is the market pricing recovery, distress, or normalized asset value?

Core insight chain:

```text
policy / rates / demand / supply / financing signal
-> sales, rent, occupancy, NOI, cap rate, or refinancing cost
-> cash flow and asset value
-> leverage, NAV, dividend / distribution safety
-> valuation rerating or derating
```

## 2. Main Sub-Sectors

### Residential Developers

Developers that acquire land, build residential projects, sell units, and recycle cash into new land or debt reduction.

Buy-side focus:

- presales
- cash collection
- land bank quality
- ASP
- sell-through
- gross margin
- debt maturity
- policy support

### Commercial Real Estate

Income-producing assets including office, retail, mixed-use, and commercial landlords.

Buy-side focus:

- rental rate
- occupancy
- renewal spread
- NOI
- cap rate
- tenant quality
- lease maturity
- refinancing cost

### REITs

Listed vehicles that own income-producing real estate and distribute cash flow.

Buy-side focus:

- NOI growth
- funds from operations
- adjusted funds from operations
- distribution yield
- payout ratio
- leverage
- cap rate
- cost of capital

### Industrial / Logistics Property

Warehouses, fulfillment centers, cold chain, logistics parks, and industrial real estate.

Buy-side focus:

- occupancy
- rent growth
- e-commerce / logistics demand
- new supply
- tenant concentration
- lease duration
- development yield

### Data Centers

Real estate and infrastructure assets serving cloud, AI, enterprise IT, and colocation demand.

Buy-side focus:

- leased megawatts
- power availability
- utilization
- backlog
- pricing
- capex
- development pipeline
- customer concentration

### Office

Office buildings and landlords exposed to employment, hybrid work, tenant downsizing, and refinancing.

Buy-side focus:

- occupancy
- leasing volume
- renewal spread
- tenant move-outs
- asset impairment
- debt maturity
- capex for repositioning

### Retail Property

Malls, outlets, shopping centers, and retail landlords.

Buy-side focus:

- tenant sales
- occupancy
- leasing spread
- foot traffic
- rent collection
- anchor tenant health
- redevelopment opportunity

### Property Management

Companies managing residential, commercial, or mixed-use properties and earning management fees or value-added service fees.

Buy-side focus:

- managed area
- fee rate
- collection quality
- value-added services
- margin
- developer customer exposure
- renewal rate

### Homebuilders

Builders that develop and sell homes, often with direct exposure to mortgage rates, affordability, land supply, and buyer incentives.

Buy-side focus:

- orders
- closings
- backlog
- ASP
- cancellation rate
- gross margin
- incentives
- land inventory

## 3. Core Formulas

```text
Property sales revenue = units sold x ASP
Rental income = rentable area x occupancy x rent per sqm
REIT value depends on NOI, cap rate, leverage, and distribution yield
NAV = asset value - net debt
```

Additional formulas:

| Segment | Formula | Buy-side interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Residential developers | units sold x ASP x cash collection rate | Tests sales recovery and liquidity conversion. |
| Commercial landlords | rentable area x occupancy x rent per sqm | Tests leasing power and rent cycle. |
| REITs | NOI / cap rate - net debt | Tests asset value, leverage, and NAV sensitivity. |
| Data centers | leased capacity x price per MW / kW | Tests AI/cloud demand and power-constrained growth. |
| Homebuilders | homes closed x ASP - construction and land cost | Tests affordability, backlog conversion, and margin. |
| Property management | managed area x fee + value-added services | Tests recurring service revenue and margin. |

## 5. Value Chain

Real estate value chain:

```text
land acquisition
-> development
-> construction
-> sales / leasing
-> property management
-> financing
-> tenants / buyers
```

Economic capture questions:

- Land acquisition: Was land bought at cycle-appropriate cost?
- Development: Are projects profitable after financing and construction cost?
- Construction: Are delays or cost overruns eroding margin?
- Sales / leasing: Is demand translating into real cash collection or signed leases?
- Property management: Is fee income recurring and collectible?
- Financing: Can debt be refinanced without value leakage or dilution?
- Tenants / buyers: Are end users financially healthy enough to sustain demand?

## 7. Financial Impact Pathway

Real estate signals must be translated into financial mechanisms.

### Sales Revenue

Signals:

- policy easing
- mortgage rate decline
- presales improvement
- homebuilder order growth
- ASP stabilization
- cash collection recovery

Financial pathway:

```text
demand / affordability / policy -> units sold and ASP -> sales revenue -> gross margin -> cash collection -> leverage
```

Key distinction:

- Contracted sales are not enough. Cash collection and delivery determine balance-sheet relief.
- Volume recovery without ASP stability may not repair margins.

### Rental Income

Signals:

- occupancy improvement
- positive renewal spread
- tenant sales recovery
- data center leasing backlog
- logistics rent growth
- office move-in / move-out trend

Financial pathway:

```text
rentable area x occupancy x rent -> rental income -> NOI -> asset value
```

### NOI

NOI improves when:

- occupancy rises
- rent per sqm increases
- tenant defaults fall
- operating expenses are controlled
- new assets stabilize

NOI weakens when:

- tenants downsize
- rent concessions increase
- utility / labor / maintenance cost rises
- vacancy increases
- asset repositioning requires higher capex

### Cap Rate

Cap rate is a valuation bridge between income and asset value.

Asset value rises when:

- NOI rises
- cap rate compresses
- rates decline
- demand for the asset class improves
- funding conditions loosen

Asset value falls when:

- NOI declines
- cap rate expands
- interest rates rise
- liquidity weakens
- asset class risk premium increases

### NAV

NAV is sensitive to asset value and leverage.

```text
NAV = asset value - net debt
```

Watch:

- appraised value assumptions
- cap rate sensitivity
- net debt
- asset impairment
- disposal price vs book value
- minority interests and off-balance-sheet obligations

### Leverage

Leverage can create equity upside or distress risk.

Watch:

- debt ratio
- net gearing
- interest coverage
- refinancing maturity
- secured asset coverage
- covenants
- liquidity runway

### Refinancing Risk

Refinancing risk rises when:

- rates rise
- asset values fall
- debt maturities cluster
- lenders reduce exposure
- interest coverage weakens
- credit rating deteriorates

Refinancing risk falls when:

- asset sales raise cash
- debt is extended
- NOI improves
- rates decline
- market access reopens

### Dividend / Distribution

Dividend and distribution quality depend on recurring cash flow, payout ratio, leverage, and capex needs.

Questions:

- Is dividend covered by FFO / AFFO or recurring cash flow?
- Is distribution funded by asset sales or debt?
- Does refinancing cost pressure future payout?
- Does capex need compete with dividends?

## 8. Buy-Side Questions

Use these questions to convert real estate signals into investment insight:

- Where are we in the property cycle?
- Are rates helping or hurting valuation?
- Is occupancy improving or deteriorating?
- Is refinancing risk manageable?
- Is NAV discounted enough?
- Is the market pricing in asset impairment?
- Which subsector has structural demand?
- Is the signal about sales, rent, occupancy, cap rate, or liquidity?
- Is the recovery visible in cash collection or only in sentiment?
- Does leverage amplify upside or create downside risk?
- Are dividends or distributions sustainable at current rates?
- Is the asset class cyclically depressed or structurally impaired?

## 9. Risks And Invalidation

Key risks:

- rate increase
- refinancing pressure
- weak sales
- occupancy decline
- asset impairment
- policy tightening
- liquidity stress
- tenant default
- oversupply
- cap rate expansion
- construction cost inflation
- delayed deliveries
- dividend / distribution cut
- land bank impairment

Invalidation triggers:

- sales volume fails to recover after policy support
- ASP declines faster than volume recovery
- cash collection remains weak
- occupancy declines or renewal spreads turn negative
- cap rates expand beyond thesis assumptions
- asset impairment reduces NAV
- refinancing terms are worse than expected
- interest coverage deteriorates
- tenant defaults rise
- dividend or distribution coverage breaks

## 10. Investment Judgment Framework

Classify real estate opportunities into one of five states.

### Long Candidate

Use when:

- property-cycle recovery is supported by sales, rent, occupancy, or financing evidence
- NAV discount is meaningful and asset value assumptions are credible
- leverage and refinancing maturity are manageable
- NOI or cash collection is improving
- dividend / distribution is covered by recurring cash flow
- market underprices normalized asset value or structural demand
- catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- sales / rent / occupancy data
- cap rate and NAV sensitivity
- debt maturity and refinancing analysis
- cash collection or NOI trend
- dividend / distribution coverage
- valuation or consensus gap

### Watchlist

Use when:

- asset quality is attractive but cycle timing is unclear
- NAV discount exists but cap rate assumptions need validation
- refinancing risk is not yet resolved
- structural demand is real but valuation is demanding

Required next step:

- track sales, cash collection, occupancy, rent, cap rate, debt maturity, and funding access.

### Too Early / Need More Evidence

Use when:

- signal is policy sentiment only
- sales recovery has not translated into cash collection
- rent or occupancy has not improved
- debt refinancing path is unclear
- asset values may still be impaired

Required next step:

- wait for presales, cash collection, leasing, refinancing, NAV, or NOI evidence.

### Avoid

Use when:

- leverage is high
- refinancing risk is severe
- occupancy is deteriorating
- asset values are overstated
- policy support is uncertain
- dividend / distribution is not covered
- oversupply pressures rent or sales

Required next step:

- avoid PM action unless leverage, liquidity, or asset-value evidence changes.

### Short Candidate

Use when:

- market prices recovery while evidence points to weak sales, falling rent, cap rate expansion, refinancing pressure, or NAV impairment
- downside catalyst is visible
- dividend / distribution cut risk is underpriced

Required evidence:

- deteriorating sales or leasing data
- rising refinancing cost
- weak cash collection
- asset impairment risk
- consensus NAV or earnings assumptions too optimistic
- valuation still pricing recovery

When this framework is used, the final report should include:

- cap rate and NAV sensitivity
- leverage and refinancing risk
- cash collection assessment
- dividend / distribution sustainability
- listed company mapping
