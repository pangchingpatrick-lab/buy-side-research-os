# Space Economy Framework

This is a buy-side space economy framework. It is designed for AST SpaceMobile, Rocket Lab, satellite communications, launch, and defense space analysis.

Use it after external data has already been collected, classified, and source-tagged.

## 1. Industry Essence

Space economy is driven by launch capability, satellite network deployment, spectrum/regulatory approval, government contracts, commercial adoption, and capital intensity.

The investable question is not "space is a large future market." The investable question is:

- Is the technology proven enough to support revenue?
- Are regulatory and spectrum rights secured?
- Are contracts funded, executable, and margin-accretive?
- Does commercial adoption justify capex and dilution?
- Is the market pricing execution risk correctly?

Core insight chain:

```text
Technical milestone
-> regulatory / spectrum approval
-> deployment cadence
-> contract or user adoption
-> revenue and backlog
-> cash burn / dilution risk
-> profitability timeline
-> valuation rerating or derating
```

## 2. Main Sub-Sectors

### Launch

Launch vehicles, reusable rockets, payload delivery, rideshare, and launch services.

Buy-side focus:

- launch success rate
- cadence
- revenue per launch
- backlog
- gross margin per launch
- reliability
- government / commercial customer mix

### Satellite Communications

Satellite broadband, mobile backhaul, enterprise connectivity, maritime, aviation, IoT, and remote-area services.

Buy-side focus:

- constellation coverage
- capacity
- users
- ARPU
- churn
- ground infrastructure
- telecom partnerships

### Direct-To-Device Connectivity

Satellite connectivity directly to standard phones or mass-market mobile devices.

Buy-side focus:

- spectrum rights
- regulatory approvals
- telecom partner commitments
- device compatibility
- coverage
- service reliability
- user monetization

### Earth Observation

Satellite imagery, geospatial data, analytics, climate, agriculture, insurance, logistics, and defense intelligence.

Buy-side focus:

- data quality
- revisit frequency
- customers
- ACV
- renewal rate
- government vs commercial mix

### Defense Space

National security, missile warning, tracking, communications, ISR, and resilient space architecture.

Buy-side focus:

- funded government contracts
- program awards
- backlog
- mission-critical status
- margin profile
- budget cycle

### Space Infrastructure

In-orbit services, space logistics, payload hosting, orbital transfer, propulsion, sensors, and infrastructure components.

Buy-side focus:

- mission validation
- contract pipeline
- component qualification
- capex and working capital
- technical risk

### Ground Stations

Ground infrastructure for communication, data downlink, network management, and control.

Buy-side focus:

- ground coverage
- latency
- capacity
- utilization
- service contracts
- partner network

### Satellite Manufacturing

Satellite buses, payloads, components, antennas, solar arrays, propulsion, and integration.

Buy-side focus:

- production scale
- cost per satellite
- yield / reliability
- delivery schedule
- customer concentration
- component bottlenecks

## 3. Core Business Models

| Model | Formula | Buy-side interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Government contracts | contract value x milestone completion | Tests funded backlog, execution, and budget priority. |
| Commercial contracts | customers x ACV x renewal rate | Tests whether the product solves a real commercial problem. |
| Users x ARPU | users x ARPU x retention | Tests direct connectivity or consumer/enterprise service monetization. |
| Capacity | capacity x utilization x price | Tests whether deployed satellites convert into monetized throughput. |
| Launch cadence | launch cadence x revenue per launch | Tests whether launch capability becomes scalable revenue. |

Key cash-flow equation:

```text
Cash runway = cash balance / quarterly cash burn
```

Space companies often rerate on milestones before profitability. Buy-side work must connect milestones to revenue, cash burn, dilution risk, and profitability timeline.

## 5. Value Chain

Space economy value chain:

```text
Launch providers
-> satellite manufacturers
-> component suppliers
-> ground infrastructure
-> telecom partners
-> government customers
-> commercial users
```

Economic capture questions:

- Launch providers: Is cadence improving and cost per launch falling?
- Satellite manufacturers: Is production scaling without reliability issues?
- Component suppliers: Are components qualified and mission-critical?
- Ground infrastructure: Does coverage and utilization support recurring service revenue?
- Telecom partners: Are partnerships contractual, exclusive, or only exploratory?
- Government customers: Is backlog funded and tied to a long-term program?
- Commercial users: Are users paying, retaining, and expanding?

## 7. Financial Impact Pathway

Space signals must be translated into financial mechanisms.

### Contract Revenue

Signals:

- government award
- funded backlog increase
- milestone completion
- contract extension
- prime / subcontractor role

Financial pathway:

```text
Contract award -> funded backlog -> milestone revenue -> gross margin -> cash conversion
```

Key question:

- Is the contract funded and executable, or only an unfunded opportunity?

### User Revenue

Signals:

- commercial launch of service
- telecom partner activation
- subscriber growth
- ARPU disclosure
- churn improvement

Financial pathway:

```text
Coverage / service availability -> users -> ARPU -> recurring revenue -> operating leverage
```

Key question:

- Are users paying and retaining, or is adoption still promotional / pilot-stage?

### Backlog

Backlog matters because many space businesses are pre-profit or capex-heavy.

Backlog is high quality when:

- funded
- tied to clear milestones
- from creditworthy customers
- margin-accretive
- executable within capacity

Backlog is lower quality when:

- unfunded
- highly contingent
- dependent on technical milestones
- margin-dilutive

### Capex

Capex is required for:

- satellite manufacturing
- constellation deployment
- launch systems
- ground infrastructure
- spectrum / regulatory preparation

Buy-side question:

- Does capex create monetizable capacity, or does it extend cash burn before demand is proven?

### Cash Burn

Cash burn is central because many space companies are milestone-driven and not yet self-funding.

Watch:

- quarterly cash burn
- cash balance
- funding runway
- capex commitments
- debt maturity
- working capital needs

### Dilution Risk

Dilution risk rises when:

- cash runway is short
- milestones slip
- commercial revenue is delayed
- capex remains high
- funding markets are tight

### Profitability Timeline

Profitability depends on:

- launch cadence and fixed-cost absorption
- satellite utilization
- recurring service revenue
- gross margin per contract
- capex moderation
- opex discipline

### Valuation

Space valuation often prices future optionality before current earnings.

Valuation rerates when:

- technology is validated
- regulatory approvals are secured
- funded contracts grow
- satellite coverage enables revenue
- cash runway improves
- commercial adoption exceeds expectations

Valuation derates when:

- milestones slip
- funding needs rise
- revenue conversion is delayed
- dilution becomes likely
- technology or regulatory risk rises

## 8. Buy-Side Questions

Use these questions to convert space signals into investment insight:

- Is the technology proven?
- Is there regulatory approval?
- Is there commercial demand?
- Is funding sufficient?
- Are milestones being met?
- Is the market pricing in execution risk correctly?
- Is the company selling launches, services, data, hardware, or government programs?
- Is backlog funded or aspirational?
- Does deployment coverage support monetization?
- Does commercial adoption justify capex?
- What event would turn the story from optionality into revenue?
- What milestone delay would force dilution?

## 9. Risks And Invalidation

Key risks:

- launch failure
- deployment delay
- regulatory rejection
- funding shortage
- dilution
- weak commercial adoption
- technology failure
- competition from incumbents
- spectrum conflict
- satellite manufacturing delays
- cost overruns
- unfunded government backlog
- telecom partner non-conversion

Invalidation triggers:

- launch failure or repeated launch delay
- satellite deployment misses timeline
- regulatory or spectrum approval denied
- telecom partner commitment weakens
- user growth or ARPU underperforms
- cash runway falls below required milestone timeline
- equity raise becomes necessary on weak terms
- funded backlog declines
- incumbent competitor launches superior service
- government program is delayed, reduced, or cancelled

## 10. Investment Judgment Framework

Classify space opportunities into one of five states.

### Long Candidate

Use when:

- technology is validated
- regulatory / spectrum path is clear
- funded backlog or commercial revenue is visible
- cash runway covers critical milestones
- market underprices execution progress
- catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- successful launch / deployment / service milestone
- funded contract or paying-user data
- cash runway analysis
- valuation / expectation gap

### Watchlist

Use when:

- milestone path is attractive but not fully de-risked
- commercial or government demand is plausible
- funding runway is adequate for near-term milestones
- valuation sensitivity is high

Required next step:

- track deployment, regulatory, contract, cash burn, and partner milestones.

### Too Early / Need More Evidence

Use when:

- technology is not proven
- regulatory approval is uncertain
- commercial demand is mostly aspirational
- funding path is unclear
- revenue model is not validated

Required next step:

- wait for technical, regulatory, contract, or user evidence.

### Avoid

Use when:

- story depends on distant optionality
- funding need is high
- dilution risk is near-term
- adoption evidence is weak
- milestones keep slipping
- valuation already prices success

Required next step:

- avoid PM action unless a concrete milestone changes risk/reward.

### Short Candidate

Use when:

- market prices successful execution
- evidence points to milestone slippage, weak demand, funding shortage, dilution, or technology failure
- downside catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- missed launch / deployment / approval milestone
- cash runway mismatch
- weak user or contract evidence
- consensus or valuation still optimistic
