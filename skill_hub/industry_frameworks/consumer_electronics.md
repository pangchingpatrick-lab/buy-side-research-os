# Consumer Electronics Framework

This is a buy-side consumer electronics industry framework. It is designed for smartphones, PCs, wearables, AR/VR, Apple supply chain, brand owners, assemblers, and component supplier analysis.

Use it after external data has already been collected, classified, and source-tagged. Do not use this framework to create generic device-market commentary.

## Boundary of this framework

- This industry framework defines the industry map, value chain, business models, core metrics, and key risks.
- It should not determine the final reasoning path.
- The final reasoning path should be selected from `skill_hub/research_methodologies/`.
- Industry framework answers "what to look at".
- Research methodology answers "how to think".

## 1. Industry Essence

Consumer electronics is driven by product cycles, replacement demand, innovation, ASP, channel inventory, supply chain efficiency, and brand power.

The investable question is not "a new device was launched." The investable question is:

- Does the product cycle change replacement behavior?
- Does innovation create pricing power or just temporary attention?
- Is demand real sell-through or only channel restocking?
- Which supplier gains content value, share, or margin?
- Is the market already pricing in the next cycle?

Core insight chain:

```text
product feature / launch / channel signal
-> replacement demand, shipment volume, ASP, or content value
-> OEM revenue and supplier orders
-> gross margin and EPS expectations
-> inventory cycle and valuation rerating / derating
```

## 2. Main Sub-Sectors

### Smartphones

High-volume device category with strong sensitivity to product cycles, replacement demand, premium mix, regional demand, and carrier/channel promotions.

Buy-side focus:

- shipment volume
- ASP
- premium mix
- replacement cycle
- sell-through vs sell-in
- component content per device
- channel inventory

### PCs

Cyclical device category driven by enterprise refresh, consumer replacement, AI PC adoption, component pricing, and channel inventory.

Buy-side focus:

- unit shipments
- enterprise demand
- consumer demand
- ASP
- CPU/GPU content
- inventory normalization
- Windows or architecture transition cycles

### Tablets

Replacement-cycle category with use-case overlap across education, productivity, media, and enterprise deployment.

Buy-side focus:

- upgrade rate
- attach rate of accessories
- ASP mix
- regional demand
- channel inventory
- enterprise / education orders

### Wearables

Devices tied to health, fitness, ecosystem lock-in, sensors, battery life, and replacement cadence.

Buy-side focus:

- active installed base
- attach rate
- sensor innovation
- ASP
- gross margin
- replacement demand
- health feature adoption

### AR/VR Devices

Early or emerging product cycle driven by display quality, compute, battery, content ecosystem, developer adoption, and price point.

Buy-side focus:

- device shipments
- developer ecosystem
- content availability
- component cost
- display / optics supply chain
- user retention
- enterprise vs consumer use cases

### Smart Home Devices

Connected speakers, displays, cameras, appliances, security devices, and home automation hardware.

Buy-side focus:

- household penetration
- attach rate
- ecosystem integration
- device margin
- subscription/service attach
- replacement cycle

### Gaming Hardware

Consoles, handheld gaming devices, peripherals, and gaming accessories.

Buy-side focus:

- console cycle timing
- hardware margin
- software attach rate
- active users
- content pipeline
- component availability
- regional demand

### Components And Modules

Display, camera modules, acoustics, haptics, connectors, batteries, RF modules, sensors, casings, PCB, memory, processors, and other bill-of-material components.

Buy-side focus:

- content value per device
- supplier share
- yield
- ASP
- gross margin
- order visibility
- customer concentration
- capacity utilization

## 3. Core Formulas

```text
Revenue = shipment volume x ASP
Gross profit = revenue x gross margin
Cycle strength depends on product upgrade, replacement demand, channel inventory, and pricing power.
```

Additional formulas:

| Segment | Formula | Buy-side interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OEM device | shipment volume x ASP | Tests unit demand and pricing power. |
| Component supplier | device shipments x content value per device x supplier share | Tests content gain and operating leverage. |
| Module maker | module shipments x module ASP | Tests product mix and customer program exposure. |
| Assembler | units assembled x assembly fee | Tests build plan, utilization, and labor/cost efficiency. |
| Wearables / accessories | installed base x attach rate x ASP | Tests ecosystem monetization and upgrade behavior. |
| Smart home / services attach | device base x attach rate x ARPU | Tests hardware-to-service monetization. |

## 5. Value Chain

Consumer electronics value chain:

```text
component suppliers
-> semiconductor suppliers
-> module makers
-> assembly manufacturers
-> brand owners
-> distributors
-> retailers
-> end consumers
```

Economic capture questions:

- Component suppliers: Does the new device increase content value per unit?
- Semiconductor suppliers: Does the cycle drive volume, ASP, or mix upgrade?
- Module makers: Does the supplier gain share, better mix, or pricing power?
- Assembly manufacturers: Do build plans improve utilization and margin?
- Brand owners: Can innovation raise ASP, gross margin, retention, or ecosystem value?
- Distributors / retailers: Is channel inventory clean enough to support reorder?
- End consumers: Is demand driven by real upgrade need or temporary discounting?

## 7. Financial Impact Pathway

Consumer electronics signals must be translated into financial mechanisms.

### Shipment Growth

Signals:

- preorder strength
- shipment forecast revisions
- supplier build-plan increases
- channel reorder
- regional market share gain
- enterprise PC refresh

Financial pathway:

```text
real demand or build plan -> shipment volume -> revenue -> utilization -> operating leverage -> EPS expectations
```

Key distinction:

- Sell-through improvement is stronger evidence than sell-in growth.
- Build-plan increases are useful but can reverse if end demand disappoints.

### ASP Change

ASP rises when:

- premium mix improves
- product features justify higher price
- discounting falls
- channel inventory is clean
- brand power strengthens

ASP falls when:

- demand requires discounting
- competition intensifies
- older models remain in the channel
- mix shifts down-market
- FX or regional demand weakens

### Margin Expansion / Compression

Margin expands when:

- ASP rises faster than component cost
- premium mix improves
- yield improves
- assembly utilization improves
- supplier mix shifts to higher-value modules
- component pricing falls for OEMs

Margin compresses when:

- component cost rises
- promotions increase
- launch ramp yield is weak
- fixed-cost absorption deteriorates
- suppliers lose share or pricing power
- FX pressure reduces reported margin

### Inventory Cycle

Inventory is central to consumer electronics investing.

Positive cycle:

```text
low channel inventory -> product launch -> sell-through improves -> reorder accelerates -> supplier orders rise
```

Negative cycle:

```text
high channel inventory -> discounting -> OEM order cuts -> supplier utilization falls -> margin pressure
```

### Supplier Order Growth

Supplier signals matter most when they show:

- content value per device rising
- share gain in a specific customer program
- order growth confirmed across multiple suppliers
- utilization improving without channel inventory build
- pricing or margin improvement, not only volume

### EPS Expectations

EPS expectations change through:

- unit growth
- ASP mix
- gross margin
- opex leverage
- supplier utilization
- inventory provisions
- FX sensitivity

### Valuation Rerating

Valuation rerates when:

- a new product cycle appears durable
- replacement cycle shortens
- ASP and margin improve together
- supplier content value rises
- channel inventory is clean
- market underestimates EPS upside

Valuation derates when:

- cycle is only restocking
- sell-through is weak
- inventory builds
- discounting rises
- product launch is delayed
- supplier order cuts expose estimate risk

## 8. Buy-Side Questions

Use these questions to convert consumer electronics signals into investment insight:

- Is this a real product cycle or temporary restocking?
- Is demand driven by innovation or discounting?
- Who has pricing power?
- Where is inventory in the channel?
- Which suppliers have the highest operating leverage?
- Is the market already pricing in the new cycle?
- Is the signal sell-through, sell-in, build plan, or supplier commentary?
- Does innovation raise ASP, units, content value, or retention?
- Which component suppliers gain share or content value?
- Is supplier revenue growth supported by confirmed OEM demand?
- Are expectations based on peak launch excitement or sustained replacement demand?
- What would make OEMs cut orders?

## 9. Risks And Invalidation

Key risks:

- weak consumer demand
- delayed product launch
- high channel inventory
- ASP decline
- component cost pressure
- competition
- FX pressure
- supply chain disruption
- poor product reception
- excessive discounting
- customer concentration
- supplier qualification failure
- order cuts after launch

Invalidation triggers:

- sell-through weaker than sell-in
- channel inventory rises above normal levels
- launch timing slips
- ASP falls despite product upgrade
- gross margin misses due to component cost or yield issues
- supplier build plans are cut
- regional demand deteriorates
- competitor pricing forces promotions
- premium mix fails to improve
- market share declines after launch

## 10. Investment Judgment Framework

Classify consumer electronics opportunities into one of five states.

### Long Candidate

Use when:

- product cycle is supported by sell-through evidence
- channel inventory is clean
- ASP or premium mix is improving
- gross margin has upside
- supplier content value or share is rising
- valuation does not fully reflect EPS upside
- catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- demand data
- channel inventory check
- OEM shipment / guidance evidence
- supplier order or content evidence
- margin and valuation sensitivity

### Watchlist

Use when:

- product cycle could be important but sell-through evidence is incomplete
- supplier exposure is promising but customer orders are not confirmed
- inventory is improving but not yet clean
- valuation depends on launch success

Required next step:

- track sell-through, channel inventory, build plans, ASP, margin, and supplier order revisions.

### Too Early / Need More Evidence

Use when:

- signal is based mainly on product rumors or early launch commentary
- revenue pathway is not visible
- supplier qualification is uncertain
- market adoption is unproven
- AR/VR or new category demand is too early to underwrite

Required next step:

- wait for shipment data, customer adoption, channel checks, filings, or earnings call confirmation.

### Avoid

Use when:

- demand is discount-driven
- channel inventory is high
- ASP is falling
- component cost pressure is rising
- supplier customer concentration is high without order visibility
- valuation already prices a strong cycle

Required next step:

- avoid PM action unless inventory, ASP, or demand data changes.

### Short Candidate

Use when:

- market prices a strong product cycle
- evidence points to weak sell-through, order cuts, inventory build, ASP pressure, or margin compression
- downside catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- channel inventory deterioration
- build-plan cuts
- supplier order revisions
- weak regional demand
- consensus estimates vulnerable to cuts

## Required Output From This Framework

When this framework is used, the final report should include:

- product-cycle classification

- shipment volume and ASP impact
- margin and component-cost pathway
- channel inventory assessment
- OEM and supplier mapping

- consensus vs variant view
