# Semiconductor Framework

This is a buy-side semiconductor industry framework. It is designed for AI infrastructure, HBM, GPU, networking, foundry, and semiconductor equipment analysis.

Use it after external data has already been collected, classified, and source-tagged.

## 1. Industry Essence

Semiconductors are driven by technology barriers, supply-demand cycles, customer qualification, capacity, yield, ASP, and capital intensity.

The investable question is not "semiconductor demand is strong." The investable question is:

- Which value-chain node captures incremental demand?
- Is the signal volume, ASP, utilization, margin, backlog, or capex?
- Is demand real end-demand or inventory restocking?
- Who controls the bottleneck?
- Is the market already pricing the earnings revision?

Core insight chain:

```text
End demand / technology transition
-> customer orders
-> shipment volume / ASP / utilization
-> gross margin
-> EPS revision
-> valuation rerating or derating
```

## 2. Main Sub-Sectors

### GPU

AI accelerators and high-performance parallel compute.

Buy-side focus:

- shipment volume
- ASP
- data center customer demand
- supply constraints
- platform ecosystem
- margin durability

### CPU

General-purpose compute for PCs, servers, and embedded systems.

Buy-side focus:

- server share
- PC cycle
- ASP
- platform transition
- competitive positioning

### Memory / HBM

DRAM, NAND, high-bandwidth memory, and AI memory subsystems.

Buy-side focus:

- bit shipment
- ASP per bit
- HBM capacity
- customer allocation
- inventory days
- contract pricing
- margin expansion / compression

### Foundry

Wafer manufacturing for fabless customers.

Buy-side focus:

- wafer starts
- wafer price
- utilization
- technology node
- yield
- advanced packaging attachment
- customer concentration

### Networking Chips

Switching, routing, Ethernet, optical DSP, retimers, and data center networking silicon.

Buy-side focus:

- AI cluster networking demand
- bandwidth transition
- customer design wins
- backlog
- ASP and margin

### ASIC

Custom silicon for hyperscalers, AI accelerators, networking, and specialized workloads.

Buy-side focus:

- customer wins
- design cycle
- ramp timing
- revenue concentration
- margin profile

### EDA

Software and IP tools used to design chips.

Buy-side focus:

- design activity
- subscription / license revenue
- customer concentration
- R&D intensity
- AI chip design complexity

### Semiconductor Equipment

Lithography, deposition, etch, metrology, inspection, and process equipment.

Buy-side focus:

- orders
- backlog
- book-to-bill
- capex cycle
- China/export-control exposure
- service revenue

### Advanced Packaging

CoWoS, 2.5D/3D packaging, chiplets, substrate, testing, and high-density interconnect.

Buy-side focus:

- packaging capacity
- yield
- qualification
- bottleneck pricing
- AI accelerator / HBM attach

## 3. Core Formula

Main formula:

```text
Revenue = shipment volume x ASP
Profit = revenue x gross margin - opex
```

Cycle position depends on:

- inventory
- pricing
- utilization
- capex

Sub-sector formulas:

| Segment | Formula | Buy-side interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| GPU / accelerator | units shipped x ASP | Tests AI capex monetization and customer demand. |
| Memory / HBM | bit shipment x ASP per bit | Tests supply tightness, pricing power, and mix. |
| Foundry | wafer starts x wafer price x utilization | Tests node demand and fixed-cost absorption. |
| Networking chips | shipment volume x ASP | Tests AI data center bandwidth bottleneck. |
| ASIC | customer programs x ramp volume x ASP | Tests hyperscaler design wins and concentration risk. |
| Equipment | tool shipments + service revenue | Tests capex cycle and capacity expansion. |
| Advanced packaging | package volume x package ASP x yield | Tests AI accelerator / HBM bottleneck monetization. |

## 5. Value Chain

Semiconductor value chain:

```text
IP / EDA
-> chip design
-> foundry
-> memory
-> equipment
-> materials
-> packaging / testing
-> cloud / datacenter / device customers
```

Economic capture questions:

- IP / EDA: Is design complexity creating durable software-like revenue?
- Chip design: Does the company own architecture, customer access, or platform lock-in?
- Foundry: Is node leadership creating utilization and pricing power?
- Memory: Is HBM changing the commodity memory cycle?
- Equipment: Is capex expanding sustainably or creating future oversupply?
- Materials: Is there scarce process exposure or commoditized supply?
- Packaging/testing: Is advanced packaging the bottleneck?
- Customers: Are orders driven by real deployment or inventory build?

## 7. Financial Impact Pathway

Semiconductor signals must be translated into financial mechanisms.

### Order Growth

Signals:

- customer design win
- hyperscaler capex increase
- foundry booking strength
- equipment backlog growth
- memory customer allocation

Financial pathway:

```text
Order growth -> backlog -> shipment schedule -> revenue visibility -> EPS expectation
```

Questions:

- Are orders cancellable?
- Is backlog priced at favorable ASP?
- Is demand broad or concentrated in one customer?

### ASP Changes

Signals:

- supply shortage
- node scarcity
- HBM allocation
- advanced packaging bottleneck
- product mix upgrade

Financial pathway:

```text
ASP increase -> revenue growth above volume -> gross margin expansion -> EPS revision
```

Negative pathway:

```text
ASP decline -> revenue pressure -> inventory write-down risk -> margin compression
```

### Margin Expansion / Compression

Margin expands when:

- utilization rises
- yield improves
- high-end product mix increases
- ASP rises faster than cost
- service revenue mix improves

Margin compresses when:

- utilization falls
- yield problems persist
- ASP declines
- inventory builds
- capex depreciation rises ahead of demand

### Inventory Cycle

Inventory cycle matters because semiconductor demand can look strong during restocking and collapse during correction.

Watch:

- inventory days
- channel inventory
- customer inventory
- lead times
- order cuts
- book-to-bill

### Capex Cycle

Capex creates future revenue for equipment suppliers but future supply risk for chipmakers.

Positive:

- capex tied to committed customer demand
- bottleneck capacity with pricing power
- advanced node or advanced packaging shortage

Negative:

- capex ahead of demand
- commodity memory overbuild
- utilization falls after supply comes online

### EPS Expectations

EPS expectations move through:

- revenue growth
- gross margin
- opex discipline
- depreciation
- inventory write-downs
- customer concentration risk
- tax / subsidy / export-control impacts

### Valuation Rerating

Valuation rerates when:

- cycle duration is longer than expected
- margin structure improves
- bottleneck status creates pricing power
- earnings revisions continue upward
- market underestimates a second-order beneficiary

Valuation derates when:

- demand is already priced in
- EPS revisions peak
- inventory or ASP turns down
- capex creates oversupply
- export controls reduce addressable market

## 8. Buy-Side Questions

Use these questions to convert semiconductor signals into investment insight:

- Where are we in the cycle?
- Is demand real or inventory restocking?
- Who has pricing power?
- Who controls the bottleneck?
- Is capex creating future oversupply?
- Is the signal already priced in?
- Is the signal about volume, ASP, mix, utilization, backlog, or margin?
- Which company has the cleanest earnings sensitivity?
- Does customer concentration improve visibility or increase risk?
- What operating metric would prove the thesis?
- What metric would show the cycle has peaked?
- Is this a direct beneficiary or a second-order beneficiary?

## 9. Risks And Invalidation

Key risks:

- inventory correction
- ASP decline
- customer order cuts
- yield problems
- export controls
- capex overbuild
- technology transition failure
- cyclicality
- customer concentration
- node delay
- packaging capacity shortage
- subsidy or policy reversal
- end-market demand slowdown

Invalidation triggers:

- inventory days rise while orders slow
- ASP falls despite bullish demand narrative
- customer order cuts or pushouts
- utilization declines
- capex rises without customer commitments
- yield issues delay product ramp
- export controls reduce shipments or tool sales
- HBM / advanced packaging bottleneck eases faster than expected
- consensus EPS revisions stop rising

## 10. Investment Judgment Framework

Classify semiconductor opportunities into one of five states.

### Long Candidate

Use when:

- demand is verified by orders, shipments, revenue, or customer commitments
- pricing power or mix improvement is visible
- utilization or backlog supports margin expansion
- company exposure is direct or bottleneck-linked
- consensus underestimates cycle duration or earnings leverage
- valuation still leaves upside

Required evidence:

- order / backlog / shipment / revenue proof
- ASP or gross margin evidence
- clear company mapping
- catalyst and invalidation trigger

### Watchlist

Use when:

- signal quality is high but financial validation is incomplete
- company exposure is plausible but not quantified
- cycle position is unclear
- valuation or consensus gap is not yet verified

Required next step:

- validate orders, ASP, inventory, margin, and consensus expectations.

### Too Early / Need More Evidence

Use when:

- evidence is technical, social, or anecdotal
- customer qualification is not confirmed
- product ramp timing is uncertain
- revenue and margin pathway is unclear

Required next step:

- wait for qualification, order, backlog, shipment, or financial disclosure.

### Avoid

Use when:

- the theme is generic semiconductor beta
- exposure is weak or indirect
- valuation already discounts the upside
- cycle risk is rising
- capex may create oversupply

Required next step:

- do not allocate PM attention unless new data changes the risk/reward.

### Short Candidate

Use when:

- consensus prices durable demand
- evidence shows inventory correction, ASP decline, order cuts, utilization drop, capex overbuild, or technology transition failure
- downside catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- leading indicator deterioration
- consensus EPS vulnerability
- valuation still optimistic
- identifiable timing catalyst
