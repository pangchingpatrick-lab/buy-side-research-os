# Healthcare Framework

This is a buy-side healthcare industry framework. It is designed for biotech, pharma, medical devices, healthcare services, GLP-1, oncology, diagnostics, insurance, CRO/CDMO, and digital health analysis.

Use it after external data has already been collected, classified, and source-tagged. Do not use this framework to create generic healthcare industry summaries.

## Boundary of this framework

- This industry framework defines the industry map, value chain, business models, core metrics, and key risks.
- It should not determine the final reasoning path.
- The final reasoning path should be selected from `skill_hub/research_methodologies/`.
- Industry framework answers "what to look at".
- Research methodology answers "how to think".

## 1. Industry Essence

Healthcare is driven by clinical efficacy, regulatory approval, reimbursement, patient volume, pricing power, pipeline quality, and commercialization capability.

The investable question is not "the treatment market is large." The investable question is:

- Does the product create clinically meaningful benefit?
- Is the evidence strong enough for approval and reimbursement?
- Can the company convert medical value into patient volume and revenue?
- Is the market already pricing approval, peak sales, or pipeline optionality?
- What is the downside if clinical, regulatory, or commercial execution fails?

Core insight chain:

```text
clinical / regulatory / reimbursement / adoption signal
-> probability of approval, patient penetration, pricing, or procedure volume
-> product revenue
-> margin, R&D, cash burn, and EPS / cash runway
-> peak sales and probability-adjusted value
-> valuation rerating or derating
```

## 2. Main Sub-Sectors

### Biotech

Companies focused on clinical-stage or early commercial therapies, often with binary trial and regulatory catalysts.

Buy-side focus:

- clinical efficacy
- safety / tolerability
- trial design
- endpoint quality
- probability of approval
- peak sales
- cash runway
- dilution risk

### Pharmaceuticals

Established drug companies with marketed products, global commercial infrastructure, and pipelines.

Buy-side focus:

- product revenue growth
- patent life
- pipeline replacement
- pricing power
- gross-to-net pressure
- operating leverage
- capital allocation

### Medical Devices

Devices, instruments, implants, robotics, consumables, and procedure-linked technologies.

Buy-side focus:

- procedure volume
- ASP
- installed base
- consumable attach
- hospital capex
- surgeon adoption
- reimbursement
- gross margin

### Healthcare Services

Providers, hospitals, clinics, pharmacies, PBMs, outpatient centers, and care delivery platforms.

Buy-side focus:

- patient visits
- reimbursement rate
- payer mix
- labor cost
- utilization
- capacity
- margin per visit
- regulatory pressure

### Diagnostics

Clinical testing, molecular diagnostics, imaging diagnostics, companion diagnostics, and lab services.

Buy-side focus:

- test volume
- price per test
- reimbursement coverage
- sensitivity / specificity
- ordering physician adoption
- lab capacity
- regulatory classification

### Digital Health

Software-enabled care, telehealth, remote monitoring, AI diagnostics, patient engagement, and healthcare workflow tools.

Buy-side focus:

- user / patient volume
- provider adoption
- reimbursement or subscription model
- retention
- clinical workflow integration
- regulatory exposure
- unit economics

### Health Insurance

Managed care, health insurers, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, employer plans, and benefit managers.

Buy-side focus:

- membership growth
- medical loss ratio
- premium yield
- utilization trend
- reimbursement policy
- risk adjustment
- operating margin
- regulatory scrutiny

### Contract Research / CDMO

CROs, clinical trial service providers, contract manufacturing, biologics manufacturing, and development partners.

Buy-side focus:

- backlog
- book-to-bill
- biopharma funding environment
- utilization
- project delays
- capacity
- margin
- customer concentration

## 3. Core Formulas

```text
Drug revenue = patients x price x treatment duration x penetration
Device revenue = procedure volume x ASP
Healthcare services revenue = patient visits x reimbursement rate
Biotech value depends on probability of approval x peak sales x time to market.
```

Additional formulas:

| Segment | Formula | Buy-side interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drug / pharma | eligible patients x penetration x net price x treatment duration | Tests patient conversion, pricing, and duration. |
| Biotech | probability of approval x peak sales / time and dilution risk | Tests probability-adjusted pipeline value. |
| Medical devices | procedure volume x ASP + consumables | Tests procedure growth and installed-base monetization. |
| Diagnostics | test volume x net price | Tests adoption and reimbursement. |
| Healthcare services | patient visits x reimbursement rate - labor / care cost | Tests utilization and margin per visit. |
| Health insurance | members x premium yield - medical claims cost | Tests medical loss ratio and pricing adequacy. |
| CRO / CDMO | backlog conversion x service price / manufacturing capacity | Tests biopharma demand and utilization. |

## 5. Value Chain

Healthcare value chain:

```text
discovery / R&D
-> clinical trials
-> regulatory approval
-> manufacturing
-> distribution
-> payers
-> hospitals / physicians
-> patients
```

Economic capture questions:

- Discovery / R&D: Is the mechanism differentiated and clinically credible?
- Clinical trials: Are endpoints meaningful and powered enough?
- Regulatory approval: Is the pathway clear and does the label support commercial value?
- Manufacturing: Can the company scale quality, supply, and cost?
- Distribution: Can the product reach the right channel efficiently?
- Payers: Is reimbursement likely at a price that supports the thesis?
- Hospitals / physicians: Will clinicians adopt this in real workflows?
- Patients: Does efficacy, safety, convenience, or cost support persistence?

## 7. Financial Impact Pathway

Healthcare signals must be translated into financial mechanisms.

### Patient Volume

Signals:

- prescription growth
- diagnosis expansion
- physician adoption
- guideline inclusion
- procedure growth
- reimbursement coverage
- supply capacity expansion

Financial pathway:

```text
eligible patients -> penetration -> prescription / procedure volume -> revenue -> margin / EPS
```

Key distinction:

- Total addressable market is not revenue.
- Revenue requires diagnosis, access, reimbursement, physician adoption, patient persistence, and supply.

### Pricing

Pricing power improves when:

- clinical benefit is differentiated
- unmet need is high
- payer budget impact is manageable
- competition is limited
- label supports broad use

Pricing weakens when:

- competitors show similar efficacy
- reimbursement restricts access
- policy reform targets the category
- gross-to-net discount rises
- safety or adherence limits value

### Reimbursement

Reimbursement is often the bridge between clinical value and financial value.

Questions:

- Which payer covers the product?
- What is the prior authorization burden?
- Is coverage broad or restricted?
- Does reimbursement support the expected net price?
- Are providers financially incentivized to adopt it?

### Product Revenue

Product revenue changes through:

- patient volume
- treatment duration
- net price
- penetration
- discontinuation rate
- supply capacity
- competitive share

### Margin

Margin expands when:

- scale improves manufacturing efficiency
- mix shifts to high-margin products
- R&D intensity falls after approval
- SG&A leverage improves
- device utilization improves

Margin compresses when:

- gross-to-net pressure rises
- launch SG&A is high
- manufacturing cost is elevated
- trial expense increases
- payer mix deteriorates

### R&D Spend

R&D can be value creating or a drain.

Positive:

- clear pipeline milestones
- differentiated mechanism
- strong trial design
- probability of approval rising

Negative:

- unfocused pipeline
- repeated trial delays
- weak endpoints
- high spend without de-risking

### Cash Burn

For biotech and early commercial healthcare companies, cash runway is central.

Watch:

- cash balance
- quarterly burn
- trial cost
- launch cost
- financing need
- dilution risk

### Approval Probability

Approval probability rises with:

- strong efficacy
- clean safety
- clinically meaningful endpoints
- consistent subgroup data
- regulatory alignment
- manufacturing readiness

Approval probability falls with:

- weak primary endpoint
- safety signal
- inconsistent data
- narrow label risk
- manufacturing issues
- regulatory delay

### Valuation Rerating

Valuation rerates when:

- trial data de-risks peak sales
- reimbursement becomes more likely
- launch trajectory beats expectations
- pipeline probability improves
- cash runway extends
- market underestimates patient penetration or duration

Valuation derates when:

- trial fails or effect size disappoints
- safety issue emerges
- approval is delayed or rejected
- reimbursement is restricted
- adoption is slower than expected
- cash runway creates dilution risk

## 8. Buy-Side Questions

Use these questions to convert healthcare signals into investment insight:

- Is the clinical data strong enough?
- Is the market size meaningful?
- Is reimbursement likely?
- Can the company commercialize effectively?
- Is the pipeline de-risked?
- Is the market already pricing in approval or peak sales?
- What is the downside if the trial fails?
- Is the endpoint clinically meaningful or only statistically significant?
- Does the label support broad adoption?
- Is pricing power sustainable under payer and policy pressure?
- Can manufacturing scale meet demand?
- Is patient persistence high enough to support peak sales?
- Is cash runway sufficient through the next catalyst?

## 9. Risks And Invalidation

Key risks:

- trial failure
- regulatory rejection
- reimbursement pressure
- pricing reform
- safety issues
- patent cliff
- weak adoption
- pipeline delay
- cash runway risk
- manufacturing bottleneck
- payer access restriction
- competitive data
- physician adoption friction
- dilution risk

Invalidation triggers:

- primary endpoint miss
- safety signal changes risk-benefit profile
- regulator rejects or delays approval
- label is narrower than expected
- reimbursement is restricted or net price is lower than expected
- prescription / procedure growth misses expectations
- discontinuation rate is high
- manufacturing capacity fails to scale
- cash runway becomes insufficient before the next catalyst
- competitor data reduces differentiation

## 10. Investment Judgment Framework

Classify healthcare opportunities into one of five states.

### Long Candidate

Use when:

- clinical data is strong and clinically meaningful
- regulatory path is clear
- reimbursement probability is high
- patient population and penetration are underappreciated
- commercialization capability is credible
- cash runway is sufficient
- valuation does not fully reflect probability-adjusted peak sales or EPS upside
- catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- clinical data quality
- regulatory and label pathway
- reimbursement evidence
- patient and penetration model
- pricing / gross-to-net assumptions
- cash runway and valuation sensitivity

### Watchlist

Use when:

- asset or product is promising but evidence is incomplete
- trial design is credible but data is pending
- reimbursement or adoption is not yet proven
- valuation depends on a near-term catalyst

Required next step:

- track clinical readout, regulatory meeting, reimbursement decision, prescription trends, procedure volume, and cash runway.

### Too Early / Need More Evidence

Use when:

- signal is preclinical or early Phase 1 only
- market size is not validated
- mechanism is interesting but efficacy is unproven
- reimbursement pathway is unclear
- commercial model is not visible

Required next step:

- wait for clinical efficacy, safety, regulatory, reimbursement, or adoption evidence.

### Avoid

Use when:

- trial data is weak
- safety risk is material
- reimbursement risk is high
- patent cliff is not offset
- pricing power is deteriorating
- cash runway is insufficient
- valuation already prices success

Required next step:

- avoid PM action unless clinical, reimbursement, cash runway, or valuation evidence changes.

### Short Candidate

Use when:

- market prices approval, broad label, or peak sales that evidence does not support
- downside catalyst is visible
- trial, reimbursement, adoption, patent, or cash runway risk is underpriced

Required evidence:

- weak or inconsistent clinical data
- regulatory or reimbursement risk
- adoption below expectations
- peak sales assumptions too optimistic
- dilution or balance-sheet pressure
- consensus estimates vulnerable to cuts
