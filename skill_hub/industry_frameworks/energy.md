# Energy Framework

This is a buy-side energy industry framework. It is designed for oil and gas, LNG, renewables, utilities, power grid, nuclear, and energy transition analysis.

Use it after external data has already been collected, classified, and source-tagged.

## Boundary of this framework

- This industry framework defines the industry map, value chain, business models, core metrics, and key risks.
- It should not determine the final reasoning path.
- The final reasoning path should be selected from `skill_hub/research_methodologies/`.
- Industry framework answers "what to look at".
- Research methodology answers "how to think".

## 1. Industry Essence

Energy is driven by commodity prices, supply-demand balance, production capacity, reserve quality, cost curve, regulation, and capital discipline.

The investable question is not "energy demand is rising." The investable question is:

- Is the commodity or power price cycle sustainable?
- Where is the company on the cost curve?
- Does the asset base create operating leverage or balance-sheet risk?
- Is capex disciplined or building future oversupply?
- Is the market pricing normalized prices, peak prices, or trough prices?

Core insight chain:

```text
Supply / demand / regulation
-> price, volume, utilization, or allowed return
-> revenue
-> margin and FCF
-> leverage and capital returns
-> valuation rerating or derating
```

## 2. Main Sub-Sectors

### Oil & Gas

Exploration and production of crude oil, natural gas, and natural gas liquids.

Buy-side focus:

- production volume
- realized price
- lifting cost
- reserve life
- decline rate
- capex discipline
- FCF yield

### LNG

Liquefied natural gas production, liquefaction, shipping, regasification, and contracts.

Buy-side focus:

- contracted capacity
- spot vs contract exposure
- liquefaction utilization
- project capex
- shipping availability
- global gas spreads

### Renewables

Renewable power generation assets, project developers, and contracted power portfolios.

Buy-side focus:

- installed capacity
- generation volume
- PPA price
- merchant exposure
- project IRR
- financing cost

### Solar

Solar modules, inverters, trackers, project developers, and solar generation.

Buy-side focus:

- module ASP
- shipment volume
- backlog
- gross margin
- subsidy exposure
- channel inventory

### Wind

Wind turbine equipment, offshore/onshore wind development, and wind power generation.

Buy-side focus:

- turbine orders
- project delays
- capacity factor
- installation cost
- service margin
- grid connection

### Battery Storage

Grid storage, residential storage, utility-scale storage, and energy management.

Buy-side focus:

- deployed capacity
- storage duration
- battery cost
- grid services revenue
- attach rate
- project IRR

### Utilities

Regulated electric and gas utilities.

Buy-side focus:

- regulated asset base
- allowed return

- load growth

### Power Grid

Transmission, distribution, substations, transformers, grid equipment, and grid modernization.

Buy-side focus:

### Uranium / Nuclear

Uranium mining, conversion, enrichment, nuclear generation, SMRs, and nuclear services.

Buy-side focus:

### Energy Services

Drilling, completion, pressure pumping, subsea, maintenance, and field services.

Buy-side focus:

- rig count

- backlog

## 3. Core Formulas

```text
Oil & gas revenue = production volume x realized price
Utility revenue = regulated asset base x allowed return
Renewables revenue = generation volume x electricity price / contracted PPA price
Profitability depends on price, cost, utilization, capex, and leverage.
```

Additional formulas:

| Segment | Formula | Buy-side interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oil & gas | production volume x realized price - lifting cost | Tests commodity price and cost-curve leverage. |
| LNG | contracted volume x contract price + spot volume x spot price | Tests contract quality and global spread exposure. |
| Renewables | generation volume x PPA / merchant price | Tests asset utilization and pricing. |
| Utilities | regulated asset base x allowed return | Tests regulatory compounding and capex recovery. |
| Solar / wind equipment | shipment volume x ASP | Tests volume, price, and margin cycle. |
| Storage | deployed capacity x utilization x service price | Tests grid monetization and project returns. |
| Energy services | activity volume x service price | Tests E&P capex cycle. |

## 5. Value Chain

Energy value chain:

```text
exploration / production
-> transportation
-> refining
-> power generation
-> grid infrastructure
-> storage
-> end customers
```

Economic capture questions:

- Exploration / production: Is the company low-cost with reserve depth?
- Transportation: Are contracts volume-based, regulated, or commodity-linked?
- Refining: Is margin driven by spreads, utilization, or product mix?
- Power generation: Is revenue merchant, contracted, or regulated?
- Grid infrastructure: Is capex recoverable through regulated returns?
- Storage: Does volatility create monetizable spreads or grid-service revenue?
- End customers: Is demand industrial, residential, data center, transportation, or policy-driven?

## 7. Financial Impact Pathway

Energy signals must be translated into financial mechanisms.

### Revenue

Signals:

- commodity price move
- production guidance
- load growth
- PPA signing
- rate case approval
- equipment order growth

Financial pathway:

```text
price / volume / allowed return -> revenue -> EBITDA -> FCF
```

### Margins

Margin expands when:

- realized prices rise faster than costs
- utilization improves
- service pricing improves
- regulated returns are approved
- equipment mix shifts to higher-margin products

Margin compresses when:

- commodity prices fall
- cost inflation rises
- utilization declines
- project delays increase cost
- regulation limits returns

### FCF

FCF is the central buy-side metric for many energy companies.

Watch:

- operating cash flow
- maintenance capex
- growth capex
- working capital
- commodity price sensitivity
- capital return policy

### Capex

Capex can create growth or destroy value.

Positive:

- low-cost reserves
- contracted renewables
- regulated grid investments
- high-return LNG projects
- bottleneck equipment capacity

Negative:

- capex based on peak commodity prices
- renewables projects with falling IRRs
- cost overruns
- overbuilding into weak demand

### Leverage

Leverage matters because commodity cycles are volatile.

Watch:

- net debt
- debt maturity
- interest coverage
- credit rating
- dividend coverage
- downside commodity price stress

### Dividend / Buyback

Capital returns are attractive when funded by sustainable FCF, not balance-sheet stretch.

Questions:

- Is dividend covered at normalized commodity prices?
- Are buybacks countercyclical or peak-cycle?
- Is management disciplined on capex?

### Valuation

Energy valuation rerates when:

- normalized commodity price assumptions rise
- FCF durability improves
- regulation supports allowed return
- capital returns increase
- balance sheet de-risks
- cost curve position improves

Valuation derates when:

- commodity prices normalize lower
- capex rises without return visibility
- leverage risk rises
- policy support reverses
- technology displacement risk increases

## 8. Buy-Side Questions

Use these questions to convert energy signals into investment insight:

- Is this commodity cycle sustainable?
- Where are we on the cost curve?
- Who has operating leverage?
- Who benefits from regulation or subsidies?
- Is capex disciplined or aggressive?
- Is the market pricing in normalized commodity prices?
- Is revenue merchant, contracted, or regulated?
- Does FCF cover dividend and buyback?
- Is capex creating future supply risk?
- Does the company have balance-sheet resilience through the cycle?
- Is the signal a price signal, volume signal, regulatory signal, or capex signal?

## 9. Risks And Invalidation

Key risks:

- commodity price decline
- regulatory change
- cost inflation
- capex overruns
- demand slowdown
- policy rollback
- balance sheet stress
- technology displacement
- utilization decline
- reserve quality disappointment
- project delay
- subsidy reduction
- grid connection bottleneck

Invalidation triggers:

- realized prices fall below thesis assumptions
- production guidance misses
- lifting cost rises materially
- project capex exceeds budget
- FCF no longer covers dividend / buyback
- debt metrics deteriorate
- allowed return reduced by regulators
- PPA pricing weakens
- policy or subsidy support is rolled back
- demand growth slows or reverses

## 10. Investment Judgment Framework

Classify energy opportunities into one of five states.

### Long Candidate

Use when:

- commodity, regulation, or demand signal is verified
- company has low-cost assets or protected regulated returns
- FCF generation is durable
- capex is disciplined
- leverage is manageable
- valuation does not fully reflect normalized earnings power
- catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- production / price / contract / regulatory proof
- FCF and leverage analysis
- capex discipline
- valuation or consensus gap

### Watchlist

Use when:

- theme is important but price, regulation, or capex evidence is incomplete
- asset quality is strong but timing is unclear
- valuation depends on normalized price assumptions

Required next step:

- track commodity price, realized price, capex, FCF, leverage, and regulatory milestones.

### Too Early / Need More Evidence

Use when:

- signal is policy, technology, or early adoption only
- project economics are not validated
- funding or permitting is unclear
- revenue pathway is not yet visible

Required next step:

- wait for project approval, contracts, production, or regulatory evidence.

### Avoid

Use when:

- capex is aggressive
- leverage is high
- cost position is weak
- commodity price assumptions are too optimistic
- policy support is uncertain
- FCF quality is poor

Required next step:

- avoid PM action unless capital discipline or price assumptions change.

### Short Candidate

Use when:

- market prices sustained commodity strength or policy support
- evidence points to price decline, cost inflation, capex overrun, leverage stress, or demand weakness
- downside catalyst is visible

Required evidence:

- deteriorating commodity, cost, capex, or regulatory indicators
- consensus estimates vulnerable to cuts
- valuation still optimistic
