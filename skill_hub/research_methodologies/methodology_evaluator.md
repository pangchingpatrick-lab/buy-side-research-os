# Methodology Evaluator

This file defines how to compare reports generated by different research methodologies.

The evaluator should not decide whether a thesis is true by itself. It compares the quality of reasoning produced by each methodology and identifies which report is most useful for PM decision-making under the current industry context.

## Evaluation Dimensions

Score each dimension from 1 to 5.

| Dimension | What Good Looks Like |
| --- | --- |
| Thesis clarity | The report states a clear investment-relevant thesis early, not a generic industry summary. |
| Evidence strength | Claims are traceable to credible sources, with verified facts separated from unverified signals. |
| Financial translation | The report explains how signals affect revenue, margin, orders, capex, EPS, FCF, or valuation. |
| Company mapping | The report maps direct, indirect, and negative listed-company exposure with economic mechanisms. |
| Variant view | The report explains what consensus likely believes and where the non-consensus view may be different. |
| Investment actionability | The report gives a PM-useful decision state such as Long candidate, Watchlist, Too early, Avoid, or Short candidate. |
| Risk / invalidation | The report states what evidence would prove the thesis wrong or force a decision change. |
| Fit with current industry | The methodology fits the industry's actual value chain, business model, metrics, and risk structure. |

## Scoring Guide

### 5 - Excellent

- reasoning is specific, traceable, and financially grounded
- company mapping explains exposure mechanisms
- variant view is explicit and non-generic
- invalidation is clear and testable
- PM can decide the next action from the output

### 4 - Strong

- core reasoning is useful
- evidence is mostly traceable
- financial path is directionally clear
- a few data gaps remain but are acknowledged

### 3 - Usable

- report has a plausible thesis
- evidence and financial translation are incomplete
- company mapping may be too broad
- PM can use it as a starting point, not a decision-ready output

### 2 - Weak

- report mostly summarizes signals or industry background
- evidence is thin
- financial translation is vague
- variant view and invalidation are underdeveloped

### 1 - Not Useful

- report is generic, unsupported, or fabricated
- no clear thesis
- no financial mechanism
- no company exposure logic
- no verification or invalidation path

## Fit With Current Industry

The evaluator must judge whether a methodology fits the current industry.

Examples:

- A signal-driven methodology may fit AI infrastructure, semiconductors, software, consumer electronics, and space economy when early signals appear before estimates update.
- A clinical-evidence methodology may fit healthcare better than a pure signal-driven methodology.
- A cycle and balance-sheet methodology may fit real estate, energy, commodities, and financials.

If the methodology does not fit the industry, the evaluator should say so directly and recommend a better reasoning path.

## Guardrails

- Do not reward confident writing if evidence is weak.
- Do not reward broad company lists without exposure logic.
- Do not reward upside claims without financial translation.
- Do not reward actionability if invalidation is missing.
- Do not treat X signals as verified evidence unless supported by primary or repeated credible sources.
