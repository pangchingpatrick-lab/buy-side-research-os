# Cross-Industry Bottleneck Framework

This reference generalizes the bottleneck method beyond AI and semiconductors.

It should be available to Skill 001-5 whenever a report starts from a broad theme and needs to identify the real constrained layer and investable beneficiary path.

## General Formula

```text
demand shock
-> constrained layer
-> scarce capability
-> underpriced beneficiary
-> falsifiable event
-> forward / benchmark audit
```

The goal is to find a research hypothesis that can be audited, not a thematic slogan.

## Workflow

### 1. Define The Demand Shock

Ask what changed:

- technology transition
- regulation
- capex cycle
- product cycle
- consumer behavior
- geopolitical or policy shift
- weather, subsidy, replacement cycle, or financing condition

The demand shock should be measurable through orders, usage, capacity additions, permits, budgets, backlog, pricing, or customer commentary.

### 2. Map The Value Chain

Break the industry into:

- customers
- platforms / integrators
- component suppliers
- equipment vendors
- materials
- logistics / service providers
- financing or regulatory gatekeepers

Mark visible consensus winners separately from less visible enablers.

### 3. Identify The Constraint

Look for:

- long lead times
- scarce input
- customer qualification
- permitting or certification
- high switching cost
- yield or manufacturing complexity
- specialized labor
- installed-base lock-in
- capacity reservation or long-term contract

A real constraint should create pricing power, delayed supply response, margin pressure, capex pull-forward, or operating leverage.

### 4. Find The Beneficiary

Classify candidate companies:

- direct owner of the scarce capability
- second-order supplier
- equipment / material vendor
- customer demand validator
- obvious large-cap proxy
- peer / comparison
- risk object or cost bearer
- watchlist only

Do not treat a company as the beneficiary merely because it is mentioned in the source.

### 5. Build A Falsifiable Event

The event must identify:

- source
- timestamp
- claim
- affected ticker or industry node
- target role
- expected mechanism
- evidence stack
- time horizon
- invalidation condition
- benchmark or peer basket

If the idea cannot be converted into a falsifiable event, keep it as a watchlist item rather than a skill rule.

### 6. Audit Price Path And Benchmark

Ask:

- Was there prior runup?
- Did the source appear before or after the move?
- Is the move absolute-only or benchmark-relative?
- Which benchmark fits the industry?
- Is the event early discovery, continuation confirmation, late validation, after-fact commentary, or context only?

## Sector Transfer Map

| Sector | Demand Shock | Likely Constraint | Beneficiary Type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AI / semiconductor | workload scaling, architecture transition | memory, packaging, optics, power, test | suppliers, equipment, materials, infrastructure |
| Energy / power | datacenter load, grid upgrade, LNG, nuclear | turbines, transformers, permits, fuel cycle | equipment, grid contractors, specialty suppliers |
| Space | satellite demand, launch cadence, defense budget | launch capacity, components, spectrum, qualification | launch, components, ground infrastructure |
| Robotics / industrials | automation adoption, labor shortage | actuators, sensors, controllers, integration | component makers, machine vision, integrators |
| Consumer electronics | product cycle, platform shift | display, battery, camera, thermal, assembly yield | component makers, materials, test vendors |
| Healthcare | procedure or drug adoption | manufacturing capacity, reimbursement, diagnostics | CDMO, diagnostics, specialty distribution |

## Guardrails

- Do not create an investment conclusion from a broad theme alone.
- Do not rank companies before ranking constrained layers.
- Do not ignore cost bearers and possible losers.
- Do not use one public claim as a permanent rule.
- Do not update memory until an evaluated report or event audit supports the lesson.
