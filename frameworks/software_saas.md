# Software SaaS Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

Software and SaaS create value by embedding into recurring workflows and compounding through retention, expansion, and operating leverage. The buy-side question is whether the product is mission-critical enough to sustain growth, pricing, and margins.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- New customer acquisition.
- Seat expansion or usage expansion inside existing customers.
- Pricing power and packaging changes.
- Net revenue retention and churn reduction.
- Product-led growth and developer adoption.
- Cloud migration, automation, AI features, cybersecurity demand.
- Enterprise budget priority and sales cycle.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- Seat-based subscription.
- Usage-based pricing.
- Enterprise license and support.
- Freemium to paid conversion.
- Platform marketplace and add-on modules.
- Professional services attached to software.

Buy-side focus:

- Is revenue recurring?
- Is growth driven by new logos, expansion, price, or usage?
- Does AI increase ARPU or reduce seat count?
- Is the product system-of-record, system-of-engagement, or a replaceable tool?

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- SaaS revenue = customers x ACV x retention
- Seat model revenue = paid seats x price per seat
- Usage model revenue = usage volume x unit price
- ARR growth = new ARR + expansion ARR - churned ARR
- Gross profit = revenue - hosting cost - support cost - third-party infrastructure cost

Key sensitivities:

- Net revenue retention.
- Gross retention.
- ACV expansion.
- Sales efficiency.
- Gross margin and cloud cost.
- R&D and S&M leverage.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- ARR, revenue growth, billings, RPO, cRPO.
- Customers, enterprise customers, paid seats, usage volume.
- ACV, ARPU, net revenue retention, gross retention, churn.
- Magic number, CAC payback, sales cycle, pipeline.
- Gross margin, operating margin, free cash flow margin.
- Rule of 40, R&D efficiency, attach rate.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Infrastructure cloud and data platforms.
2. Developer platform and product engineering.
3. Application layer and workflow ownership.
4. Distribution: direct sales, PLG, channel, marketplace.
5. Enterprise buyers and end users.
6. Integrations, data, and ecosystem partners.

Questions:

- Who owns the workflow and data?
- Is the product a budget line or a feature inside another platform?
- Does distribution advantage lower CAC?

## 7. Listed Company Mapping

Map companies by software category and financial model:

| Role | Positive exposure | Negative exposure | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mission-critical platform | Retention, pricing, module expansion | Budget scrutiny | ARR, NRR, RPO, customer growth |
| Usage-based platform | Usage growth | Consumption slowdown | Usage metrics, billings, cloud cost |
| AI-enabled software | ARPU uplift, automation | Seat compression, compute cost | Attach rate, price uplift, gross margin |
| Legacy software | Migration base, maintenance cash flow | Cloud disruption | Renewal rate, migration pace |

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Signal -> product adoption -> customers / ACV / usage / retention -> ARR and billings -> margin and FCF -> valuation.

Examples:

- AI feature launch -> attach rate -> ARPU uplift -> compute cost -> gross margin.
- Developer adoption -> PLG conversion -> lower CAC -> operating leverage.
- Enterprise slowdown -> longer sales cycle -> billings miss -> multiple compression.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus may extrapolate ARR growth or focus on AI narratives. Variant view should test:

- Whether AI features create paid uplift or just defend retention.
- Whether usage growth is durable or cyclical.
- Whether NRR is masking weak new logo growth.
- Whether gross margin is at risk from infrastructure cost.
- Whether valuation assumes growth reacceleration without evidence.

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Churn or NRR deterioration.
- Sales cycle elongation.
- Usage slowdown.
- Price resistance.
- Cloud cost or AI inference cost pressuring margin.
- Competitive bundling by larger platforms.
- Security incident or platform reliability issue.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

- Long candidate: durable retention, clear expansion path, improving sales efficiency, and valuation not fully reflecting operating leverage.
- Watchlist: product signals strong but ARR, billings, or NRR validation missing.
- Too early / Need more evidence: adoption is developer or X-led only.
- Avoid: growth is decelerating with weak margin leverage and expensive valuation.
- Short candidate: consensus expects reacceleration but evidence shows churn, sales friction, or commoditization.
