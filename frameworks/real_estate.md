# Real Estate Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

Real estate monetizes land, buildings, occupancy, rent, sales, service fees, and financing structure. The buy-side question is how property cash flow, asset values, leverage, and policy interact to create or destroy equity value.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- Property sales volume and ASP.
- Rental occupancy and rent growth.
- Interest rates and financing availability.
- Land supply, inventory, and policy.
- Urban migration, household formation, office demand, logistics demand.
- Asset recycling, REIT growth, property management scale.
- Balance sheet repair or distress.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- Property development: units sold x ASP minus land and construction cost.
- Rental assets: leasable area x occupancy x rent.
- REITs: property NOI and distribution yield.
- Property management: managed area x fee rate.
- Brokerage / platform: transaction volume x commission rate.
- Services: service contracts and value-added fees.

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- Development revenue = contracted sales area x ASP
- Rental income = leasable area x occupancy x rent
- Property management revenue = managed area x fee per area + value-added services
- Brokerage revenue = transaction volume x commission rate
- NOI = rental income - property operating expenses
- Equity value = asset value - net debt, adjusted for cash flow and refinancing risk

Key sensitivities:

- Sales volume.
- ASP.
- Rent and occupancy.
- Cap rate.
- Interest rate.
- Leverage and refinancing cost.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- Contracted sales, GFA sold, ASP, sell-through rate.
- Land bank, land cost, inventory, construction progress.
- Occupancy, rent per square meter, renewal spread.
- NOI, cap rate, distribution yield.
- Debt maturity, net gearing, interest coverage, cash balance.
- Managed area, fee rate, collection rate.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Land acquisition and permitting.
2. Financing and construction.
3. Sales / leasing / operations.
4. Property management and services.
5. Capital recycling, REITs, securitization.
6. End buyers, tenants, investors, lenders, regulators.

Questions:

- Is the signal about sales, rent, occupancy, financing, policy, or asset value?
- Does it affect income statement, cash flow, or balance sheet first?
- Is leverage amplifying upside or downside?

## 7. Listed Company Mapping

| Role | Revenue mechanism | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developer | Sales volume x ASP | Contracted sales, land cost, inventory, debt |
| Landlord / REIT | Rent x occupancy | Leasing data, rent spread, cap rate |
| Property manager | Managed area x fee | Managed GFA, fee rate, collection |
| Broker / platform | Transaction volume x take rate | Transaction data, commission rate |
| Construction / materials supplier | Project volume x ASP | Starts, completions, order data |

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Signal -> transaction / rent / policy / financing change -> cash collection or NOI -> debt capacity -> asset value and equity valuation.

Examples:

- Sales recovery -> cash collection -> debt repayment -> lower distress risk.
- Rate cut -> cap rate compression -> asset value uplift.
- Occupancy decline -> NOI pressure -> distribution risk.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus may focus on policy headlines or property prices. Variant view should test:

- Whether policy changes revive transactions or just stabilize sentiment.
- Whether cash collection improves enough to reduce leverage risk.
- Whether rental income is resilient relative to asset valuation.
- Whether book value overstates recoverable asset value.
- Whether service-fee businesses are more durable than development profits.

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Sales recovery fails.
- ASP declines or discounting intensifies.
- Refinancing blocked or debt maturity pressure rises.
- Occupancy falls or rent declines.
- Policy support weaker than expected.
- Construction delays or delivery obligations.
- Asset impairment.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

- Long candidate: verified sales/rent/cash-flow improvement, manageable leverage, and market underestimates balance sheet repair.
- Watchlist: policy or demand signal positive but cash collection not yet proven.
- Too early / Need more evidence: sentiment signal without transaction or financing data.
- Avoid: leverage risk dominates upside.
- Short candidate: market prices recovery but evidence shows weak sales, falling rent, or refinancing stress.
