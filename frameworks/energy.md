# Energy Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

Energy converts resources, infrastructure, capacity, and regulation into commodity sales, power generation, or contracted returns. The buy-side question is how volume, price, utilization, regulation, and capital intensity flow into cash flow.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- Electricity demand, industrial load, AI data center load.
- Commodity prices: oil, gas, coal, power, carbon, lithium where relevant.
- Installed capacity and utilization.
- Grid investment, storage deployment, transmission constraints.
- Policy, subsidies, tariffs, and environmental regulation.
- Project pipeline, permitting, capex, and financing cost.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- Power generation: generation volume x electricity price.
- Renewable projects: installed capacity x utilization x contracted tariff.
- Oil and gas: production volume x realized price.
- Energy infrastructure: capacity contracts, tolling fees, regulated returns.
- Storage: capacity payments, arbitrage spread, grid services.
- Equipment suppliers: units or capacity shipped x ASP.

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- Power revenue = generation volume x electricity price
- Renewable revenue = installed capacity x capacity factor x tariff
- Oil and gas revenue = production volume x realized price
- Infrastructure revenue = contracted capacity x fee
- Equipment revenue = shipped capacity x unit price
- Profit = revenue - fuel cost - operating cost - depreciation - financing cost

Key sensitivities:

- Commodity price.
- Capacity factor.
- Utilization.
- Project IRR.
- Financing cost.
- Capex per unit.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- Installed capacity, generation volume, capacity factor.
- Realized price, tariff, PPA price, merchant exposure.
- Production volume, reserve life, lifting cost.
- Project backlog, capex, COD timing.
- EBITDA, free cash flow, leverage, dividend capacity.
- Grid connection, curtailment, storage duration.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Resource extraction or equipment manufacturing.
2. Generation assets, renewable assets, storage, grid.
3. Transmission and distribution.
4. Retail supply, industrial customers, data centers.
5. Regulators, utilities, commodity markets.

Questions:

- Is revenue merchant, regulated, or contracted?
- Does demand growth translate into pricing, utilization, or capex?
- Who earns the return: generator, grid owner, equipment supplier, or fuel producer?

## 7. Listed Company Mapping

| Role | Revenue mechanism | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Power generator | Generation x power price | Load growth, tariffs, generation mix |
| Renewable developer | Capacity x contracted tariff | Project pipeline, PPAs, COD schedule |
| Grid / utility | Regulated asset base x allowed return | Capex plan, rate cases, regulation |
| Oil and gas producer | Volume x realized price | Production guidance, cost, reserves |
| Equipment supplier | Shipments x ASP | Orders, backlog, pricing, capacity |

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Signal -> demand / policy / price / capacity -> utilization or realized price -> EBITDA / FCF -> valuation and balance sheet capacity.

Examples:

- AI data center load -> power demand -> grid capex and power price -> utility earnings or generator cash flow.
- Storage adoption -> equipment shipments -> revenue and margin mix.
- Oil price move -> realized price -> cash flow and capital returns.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus may focus on commodity spot prices or policy headlines. Variant view should test:

- Whether demand is local and grid-constrained.
- Whether contracted returns are more durable than market expects.
- Whether capex inflation erodes project IRR.
- Whether supply additions cap pricing upside.
- Whether balance sheet risk is underpriced.

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Commodity price reversal.
- Policy or subsidy change.
- Permitting delays.
- Capex inflation and financing cost increase.
- Grid curtailment or connection delays.
- Lower utilization or weaker demand.
- Balance sheet stress.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

- Long candidate: verified demand or price uplift, clear asset exposure, attractive IRR or cash flow, and consensus underestimates durability.
- Watchlist: strong thematic signal but project timing or financial impact unclear.
- Too early / Need more evidence: demand signal not linked to contracted assets or pricing.
- Avoid: capital intensity high and returns uncertain.
- Short candidate: consensus prices growth but evidence points to weak utilization, falling prices, or capex overruns.
