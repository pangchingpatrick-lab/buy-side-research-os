# Space Economy Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

The space economy monetizes orbital infrastructure, launch access, satellite networks, data, connectivity, and government or commercial contracts. The buy-side question is whether technical progress converts into recurring revenue, contract backlog, lower launch cost, or strategic market share.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- Launch cost decline and launch cadence improvement.
- Satellite manufacturing scale and reliability.
- Government and defense spending.
- Broadband and direct-to-device connectivity demand.
- Earth observation, mapping, weather, maritime, aviation, and IoT use cases.
- National security and sovereign space programs.
- Reusable launch, miniaturization, and ground-station infrastructure.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- Launch services: launches x price per launch.
- Satellite connectivity: users x ARPU or contracts x annual value.
- Earth observation / data: subscribers or contracts x data / analytics price.
- Government contract revenue: contract awards x delivery milestones.
- Hardware and subsystem sales: units x ASP.
- Managed service platforms: recurring service fees plus equipment sales.

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- Connectivity revenue = users x ARPU x retention
- Government revenue = contract value x milestone completion rate
- Launch revenue = launches x price per launch
- Data revenue = customers x ACV x renewal rate
- Hardware revenue = units shipped x ASP
- Gross profit = revenue - launch cost - satellite manufacturing cost - ground network cost - service cost

Key sensitivities:

- Launch cadence.
- Satellite utilization.
- Subscriber growth and churn.
- Contract win rate.
- Capex intensity.
- Constellation replacement cycle.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- Launches completed, launch backlog, payload capacity.
- Satellites deployed, active satellites, coverage, uptime.
- Subscribers, ARPU, churn, data consumption.
- Contract backlog, funded backlog, award pipeline.
- Capex, satellite manufacturing cost, launch cost per kg.
- Ground station coverage, latency, bandwidth.
- Regulatory approvals and spectrum rights.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Components, semiconductors, sensors, payloads.
2. Satellite manufacturing and integration.
3. Launch vehicles and launch services.
4. Ground stations and network infrastructure.
5. Spectrum and regulatory approvals.
6. Connectivity / data / defense service providers.
7. End users: government, defense, enterprise, telecom, consumer.

Buy-side questions:

- Is the company selling hardware, launch capacity, data, connectivity, or strategic contracts?
- Is the revenue recurring or project-based?
- Is capex front-loaded with uncertain utilization?

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Signal -> contract / deployment / user adoption -> backlog or recurring revenue -> capex and margin profile -> cash flow and valuation.

Examples:

- New government award -> backlog -> milestone revenue -> margin and cash conversion.
- Satellite deployment acceleration -> coverage improvement -> subscriber growth -> ARPU and churn.
- Launch cadence improvement -> revenue capacity and cost absorption.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus may focus on total addressable market and technical ambition. Variant view should test:

- Whether contracts are funded and executable.
- Whether unit economics work after capex and replacement costs.
- Whether subscriber demand supports constellation capacity.
- Whether regulatory and spectrum constraints are underestimated.
- Whether revenue is recurring or one-time.

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Launch failure or reliability issues.
- Delayed regulatory approval or spectrum constraints.
- Contract cancellation or unfunded backlog.
- Subscriber growth below plan.
- Capex overruns and dilution risk.
- Technology obsolescence or constellation replacement burden.
- Geopolitical restrictions.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

- Long candidate: verified contract backlog or user growth, improving unit economics, and underappreciated recurring revenue.
- Watchlist: strong technical progress but weak financial disclosure.
- Too early / Need more evidence: ambition is high but contracts, cadence, or adoption are unverified.
- Avoid: capex intensity overwhelms revenue visibility.
- Short candidate: market prices strategic value but evidence shows execution delays, weak utilization, or funding gaps.
