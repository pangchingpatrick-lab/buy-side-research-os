# AI Industry Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

AI creates value when model capability is embedded into real workflows and changes cost, speed, quality, or automation rates. The buy-side question is not whether AI is impressive, but where model capability turns into paid usage, durable workflow lock-in, and measurable financial impact.

Key lens:

- Model capability must connect to workflow adoption.
- Workflow adoption must connect to usage volume, seats, or contracts.
- Usage must connect to revenue, margin, compute cost, and retention.
- Infrastructure demand must connect to orders, capacity, utilization, power, networking, or chips.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- Model capability improvement: accuracy, reasoning, multimodal, latency, context length, tool use.
- Unit cost decline: inference cost per token, training efficiency, hardware utilization.
- Workflow penetration: developer, enterprise, consumer, vertical industry adoption.
- Distribution: cloud platforms, enterprise software, consumer apps, developer ecosystems.
- Trust and governance: security, privacy, compliance, auditability.
- Infrastructure scaling: GPUs, accelerators, networking, memory, power, data centers.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- API usage-based model: customers pay for tokens, calls, compute time, or workload volume.
- Subscription model: users or seats pay recurring fees for AI features.
- Enterprise contract model: annual commitments, private deployments, support, and customization.
- Infrastructure rental model: compute capacity, model serving, cloud instances, storage, networking.
- Vertical application model: AI embedded into a specific workflow with business outcome pricing.

Buy-side focus:

- Is monetization tied to actual usage or just experimentation?
- Does the product own workflow distribution or rely on another platform?
- Does gross margin improve as inference cost declines?
- Does usage expand inside customers after the first deployment?

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- AI application revenue = active users or seats x ARPU x retention
- Usage revenue = usage volume x unit price
- Enterprise AI revenue = contract count x ACV x renewal rate
- AI infrastructure revenue = capacity sold x utilization x price per unit
- Gross profit = revenue - inference cost - data cost - cloud / hardware cost - support cost

Main sensitivities:

- Usage volume growth vs unit price compression.
- Inference cost per task.
- Compute utilization.
- Churn and expansion rate.
- Customer acquisition cost and sales cycle.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- Active users, paid seats, enterprise customers, net revenue retention.
- Tokens, API calls, inference requests, compute hours, workload volume.
- Latency, accuracy, hallucination rate, task completion rate.
- Cost per token, cost per inference, GPU utilization, model serving efficiency.
- ACV, ARR, gross margin, payback period, renewal rate.
- Data center capacity, accelerator supply, networking bandwidth, power availability.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Chips and accelerators.
2. Memory, networking, optical interconnect, servers, cooling, power.
3. Cloud and data center platforms.
4. Foundation models and model tooling.
5. Developer infrastructure and orchestration.
6. Enterprise software and vertical AI applications.
7. End customers and workflow owners.

Questions:

- Which layer captures pricing power?
- Which bottleneck controls deployment speed?
- Which layer is becoming commoditized?
- Which layer owns customer distribution and usage data?

## 7. Listed Company Mapping

Map listed companies by role, not by hype exposure:

| Role | What to map | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct infrastructure beneficiary | Chips, memory, networking, data centers, cloud | Orders, revenue mix, capex, utilization, backlog |
| AI platform beneficiary | Cloud AI platform, model platform, developer tooling | Usage, customer count, attach rate, gross margin |
| Application beneficiary | AI features embedded in workflow software | Paid adoption, ARPU uplift, retention, productivity proof |
| Indirect beneficiary | Power, cooling, equipment, components | Capacity demand, customer exposure, pricing |
| Potential loser | Labor-intensive software, weak workflow ownership, commoditized tools | Churn, pricing pressure, margin compression |

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Translate signals through:

Signal -> adoption -> usage / seats / capacity -> revenue -> gross margin -> operating leverage -> valuation.

For infrastructure:

AI demand -> data center capex -> accelerator / memory / networking orders -> revenue backlog -> margin mix -> earnings revision.

For applications:

Workflow adoption -> paid conversion -> ARPU / seat expansion -> retention -> ARR growth -> margin impact after compute cost.

Do not claim earnings impact without linking the signal to customer adoption, pricing, usage, or supply constraints.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus often focuses on broad AI demand. Variant view must be more precise:

- Market may overestimate model-layer durability and underestimate distribution owners.
- Market may underestimate infrastructure bottlenecks such as power, networking, memory, cooling, or inference latency.
- Market may overestimate revenue if usage is trial-driven and not workflow-critical.
- Market may underestimate margin risk from high inference cost.
- Market may misprice second-order beneficiaries if evidence appears first in technical or operating signals.

Required output:

- What is already priced in?
- Which layer has underestimated earnings sensitivity?
- Which adoption metric would prove consensus wrong?

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Usage does not convert into paid revenue.
- Unit price declines faster than usage growth.
- Inference cost prevents margin expansion.
- Enterprise adoption stalls due to security, accuracy, or workflow integration limits.
- Open-source or model commoditization erodes pricing power.
- Infrastructure orders are pulled forward and not sustainable.
- Power, supply, or regulation constraints delay deployment.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

Use this decision logic:

- Long candidate: verified paid adoption, clear company exposure, improving usage economics, and consensus underestimates earnings impact.
- Watchlist: strong early signals but missing financial validation or company-level exposure.
- Too early / Need more evidence: mostly X or developer signals without customer, revenue, or margin proof.
- Avoid: adoption is generic, monetization unclear, or priced in without earnings support.
- Short candidate: market prices durable AI benefit but evidence shows weak retention, margin drag, commoditization, or unsustainable capex.

Minimum evidence before upgrading:

- At least one primary or financial source validating adoption or demand.
- Clear revenue/profit formula.
- Company mapping with evidence-supported exposure.
- Concrete catalyst and invalidation trigger.
