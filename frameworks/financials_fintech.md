# Financials Fintech Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

Financials and fintech monetize money movement, credit intermediation, risk pooling, transactions, assets, and financial infrastructure. The buy-side question is how volume, spread, take rate, risk cost, regulation, and operating leverage translate into earnings quality.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- Transaction volume and payment penetration.
- Loan growth, deposit growth, and net interest margin.
- Asset prices, trading volume, and wealth management flows.
- Insurance premiums, loss ratio, and investment income.
- Take rate, service fees, and platform monetization.
- Credit quality and risk cost.
- Regulation, capital requirements, and interest-rate cycle.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- Bank: earning assets x net interest margin + fees - credit cost.
- Payments / fintech: transaction volume x take rate + service fees.
- Broker / exchange: trading volume x fee rate + data / listing / clearing fees.
- Wealth platform: AUM x fee rate.
- Insurance: premiums + investment income - claims - expenses.
- Lending platform: loan originations x fee / spread minus loss costs.

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- Bank revenue = earning assets x NIM + fee income
- Bank profit = revenue - operating cost - credit cost - tax
- Payments revenue = transaction volume x take rate + service fees
- Broker / exchange revenue = trading volume x fee rate + recurring services
- Wealth revenue = AUM x fee rate
- Insurance profit = premiums - claims - expenses + investment income

Key sensitivities:

- Volume growth.
- Take rate.
- NIM and rate sensitivity.
- Credit cost and loss ratio.
- Capital ratio.
- Operating leverage.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- Transaction volume, TPV, take rate, active users, merchant count.
- Loan book, deposit growth, NIM, non-performing loan ratio, provisions.
- AUM, net flows, fee rate, client assets.
- Trading volume, market share, clearing volume.
- Premiums, combined ratio, loss ratio, investment yield.
- CAC, fraud loss, delinquency, charge-off.
- Capital adequacy and liquidity metrics.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Customer acquisition and distribution.
2. Data, underwriting, compliance, and risk controls.
3. Payment rails, banking licenses, clearing, custody.
4. Balance sheet funding or capital partners.
5. Product monetization: spread, fees, take rate, service charges.
6. Regulation and capital supervision.

Questions:

- Is revenue volume-driven, spread-driven, fee-driven, or balance-sheet-driven?
- Who holds credit risk?
- Does regulation cap profitability or protect incumbents?

## 7. Listed Company Mapping

| Role | Revenue mechanism | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bank | Assets x NIM + fees | Loan growth, deposits, NIM, credit cost |
| Payments / fintech | TPV x take rate + fees | TPV, take rate, active users, fraud loss |
| Broker / exchange | Trading volume x fee rate | Volume, market share, fee schedule |
| Wealth manager | AUM x fee rate | Net flows, AUM, fee margin |
| Insurer | Premiums and investment income | Combined ratio, premium growth, yield |

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Signal -> volume / spread / take rate / risk cost change -> revenue and margin -> capital or loss impact -> earnings and valuation.

Examples:

- Payment adoption -> TPV growth -> take-rate revenue -> operating leverage.
- Rate change -> NIM expansion or compression -> bank earnings.
- Credit deterioration -> provisions -> ROE decline.
- Market volume spike -> broker/exchange fee revenue.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus may focus on top-line volume or rate direction. Variant view should test:

- Whether take rate is sustainable under competition.
- Whether credit cost is lagging and underprovided.
- Whether NIM benefit is offset by deposit cost.
- Whether regulatory risk is underpriced.
- Whether operating leverage is real after fraud, compliance, and acquisition cost.

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Credit losses rise.
- Take rate compression.
- Regulatory penalties or license restrictions.
- Funding cost increases.
- Fraud or compliance failures.
- Trading volume normalizes.
- Capital shortfall or liquidity stress.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

- Long candidate: verified volume/spread growth, controlled risk cost, operating leverage, and consensus underestimates earnings durability.
- Watchlist: strong user or volume signal but monetization or risk cost unclear.
- Too early / Need more evidence: adoption signal without financial metric validation.
- Avoid: growth depends on subsidized pricing or unmanaged credit risk.
- Short candidate: market prices growth but evidence shows take-rate pressure, credit deterioration, or regulatory risk.
