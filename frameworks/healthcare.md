# Healthcare Framework

## 1. Industry Essence

Healthcare monetizes clinical outcomes, patient access, procedure volume, reimbursement, pricing, and regulatory approval. The buy-side question is whether medical value can become reimbursed revenue with durable margins and acceptable risk.

## 2. Core Growth Drivers

- Patient population and diagnosis rate.
- Clinical efficacy and safety.
- Regulatory approval and label expansion.
- Reimbursement and pricing.
- Physician adoption and hospital purchasing.
- Procedure volume and utilization.
- Pipeline progress, trial readouts, and competitive data.

## 3. Business Model

Common models:

- Pharma / biotech: patients x price x duration of therapy.
- Medtech: procedure volume x ASP or installed base x consumables.
- Diagnostics: test volume x price.
- Healthcare services: patients / visits x reimbursement rate.
- Healthcare platforms: members / customers x service fee.
- Hospitals: patient volume x reimbursement, adjusted by payer mix and cost.

## 4. Core Revenue/Profit Formula

Core formulas:

- Drug revenue = eligible patients x penetration x net price x treatment duration
- Medtech revenue = procedure volume x device ASP + consumables
- Diagnostics revenue = test volume x price
- Services revenue = patient visits x reimbursement rate
- Gross profit = revenue - manufacturing cost - service delivery cost - rebates

Key sensitivities:

- Approval probability.
- Label size.
- Reimbursement rate.
- Net price after rebates.
- Adoption curve.
- Competitive intensity.

## 5. Key Operating Metrics

- Patient population, diagnosis rate, prescription volume.
- Trial endpoints, efficacy, safety, adverse events.
- Approval status, label, reimbursement coverage.
- Procedure volume, installed base, utilization.
- Net price, gross-to-net adjustment, payer mix.
- R&D spend, pipeline milestones, cash runway.
- Hospital occupancy, visits, revenue per patient.

## 6. Value Chain / Supply Chain

Value chain:

1. Discovery, clinical trials, regulatory approval.
2. Manufacturing and quality control.
3. Payer reimbursement and pricing negotiation.
4. Physicians, hospitals, pharmacies, distributors.
5. Patients and care delivery.
6. Post-market monitoring and lifecycle management.

Questions:

- Is the bottleneck clinical data, approval, reimbursement, or adoption?
- Does the company own pricing power or depend on payer acceptance?
- Is revenue recurring, episodic, or milestone-based?

## 7. Listed Company Mapping

| Role | Revenue mechanism | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pharma / biotech | Patients x net price x duration | Trial data, approval, prescription trends |
| Medtech | Procedure volume x ASP | Procedure data, hospital adoption, installed base |
| Diagnostics | Test volume x price | Guideline adoption, reimbursement, volume |
| Services / hospitals | Patient volume x reimbursement | Utilization, payer mix, labor cost |
| Platform / insurer / fintech health | Members x fee or spread | Members, medical loss ratio, retention |

## 8. Financial Impact Pathway

Signal -> clinical / regulatory / reimbursement / utilization change -> patient volume or price -> revenue -> margin / cash flow -> valuation.

Examples:

- Positive trial readout -> approval probability -> peak sales estimate -> valuation rerating.
- Reimbursement expansion -> patient access -> prescription volume.
- Procedure recovery -> device volume -> operating leverage.

## 9. Consensus vs Variant View

Consensus may focus on headline trial success or market size. Variant view should test:

- Whether trial endpoints translate into physician adoption.
- Whether reimbursement supports the assumed net price.
- Whether safety or tolerability limits penetration.
- Whether competitor data changes peak share.
- Whether valuation already discounts approval and launch success.

## 10. Risks and Invalidation Triggers

- Trial failure or weaker-than-expected efficacy.
- Safety signal.
- Regulatory delay or rejection.
- Reimbursement denial or price pressure.
- Slower physician adoption.
- Competitive therapy with better profile.
- Manufacturing or quality issue.

## 11. Buy-Side Investment Judgment Framework

- Long candidate: validated clinical advantage, clear reimbursement path, identifiable patient population, and underappreciated revenue potential.
- Watchlist: promising data but approval, pricing, or adoption uncertain.
- Too early / Need more evidence: preclinical or anecdotal evidence only.
- Avoid: medical value unclear or commercial path weak.
- Short candidate: market prices high peak sales but evidence shows safety, reimbursement, or adoption risk.
